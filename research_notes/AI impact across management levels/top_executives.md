# Top Executives (CEOs, C-suite, SVPs, Board Directors): How Generative/Agentic AI Changed Their Workload, Information Volume, Decision Load, and Role — 2026 Publications (Jan–Oct 2026)

Scope note for the writer: Everything below is drawn from items published January–October 2026 unless explicitly flagged "2025 context." Several 2026 publications report fieldwork or turnover data from late 2025; these are flagged inline. Many sources are vendor- or consultancy-sponsored self-report surveys; observed (non-survey) data is scarce and is called out where it exists (Russell Reynolds/Challenger turnover counts, Revelio/Live Data job-posting data, BCG's S&P 1200 turnover model).

## Key Question 1: What do 2026 CEO/C-suite surveys say about executives' personal AI use, workload, and information overload?

### Takeaway
The large 2026 CEO surveys (PwC n=4,454; KPMG n=1,300+; IBM n=2,000; BCG n=640 CEOs; Gartner n=203–330; Egon Zehnder n=843) overwhelmingly measure what CEOs *expect* or *invest in* with AI, not how much they personally use it. The one large 2026 dataset on executives' own usage (Stanford/NBER, n>6,000 executives) finds that nearly 70% of CEOs/CFOs/senior executives use AI less than one hour per week and 28% never do — slightly *less* than their own employees. Workload is measured only indirectly: 57% of CEOs say near-term issues consume an outsized share of their time (BCG), 72% find it increasingly hard to decide which disruptive forces to prioritize (AlixPartners), and CEOs spend roughly half their time on decisions with a horizon under one year (PwC).

Tier gap: only 58% of vice presidents feel confident using AI without putting company data at risk vs. 85% of directors; 39% of VPs had reworked team structures/workflows around AI vs. 71% of directors. — [General Assembly/EZRA press release via AccessNewswire, May 19, 2026](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/business-and-professional-services/business-leaders-still-primarily-use-ai-for-simple-tasks-study-f-1167022); also [IT Brief UK coverage](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/leaders-use-ai-mainly-for-routine-tasks-survey-finds)

### Inferences
- The "AI mandate from above, low use at the top" pattern (Bloom/NBER; GA/EZRA) suggests that in 2026 executives' *workload* from AI is mostly governance, investment and communication load rather than hands-on tool use — consistent with Dataiku's 89% of US CEOs reporting increased involvement in AI decisions and BCG's doubling of CEOs as "main AI decision maker."
- The VP-vs-director gap (GA/EZRA) and the executive-vs-worker hours-saved gap (Section) point to AI's time benefits accruing to people who delegate (executives) more than to those who execute; this is self-reported and may reflect optimism bias rather than measured time.
- Egon Zehnder's "endurance" finding (82%), AlixPartners' prioritization difficulty (72%, rising), and PwC's time horizon data (half of CEO time on <1-year decisions) together indicate rising *decision* load even where AI *information* load is not directly measured.

### Gaps
- No 2026 survey isolates CEOs' (as opposed to broader "senior executives") daily/weekly personal AI usage. The Bloom/NBER sample mixes CEOs, CFOs and other senior leaders.
- No 2026 source quantifies executive working hours (the Porter–Nohria 62.5 hrs/week figure cited in HBR Feb 2026 is a decade old). "Information overload" is not directly measured in any 2026 CEO survey found.
- PwC's and Gartner's full reports were inaccessible (403/paywall); figures rely on press releases and reputable secondary coverage.
- Conference Board's corrected AI-priority figures (Feb 2026 correction) are behind a login and were not retrieved.
- Deloitte/Fortune CEO Survey: no 2026 edition found (latest found was Jan 2025 — 2025 context, not used). Heidrick & Struggles: no 2026 CEO/AI survey found. Spencer Stuart 2026 mid-year S&P 500 transitions report not found.

## Key Question 2: Is there a measured 2026 gap between how executives perceive AI's benefits/workload effects and how frontline employees and managers experience them?

### Takeaway
Yes — at least nine 2026 datasets quantify a leader–worker "AI perception gap," consistently in the same direction: executives report far larger benefits, enthusiasm, trust, readiness and productivity gains than employees or middle managers do. Gaps range from ~2x (Bloom/NBER productivity forecasts: 2.3% vs 0.9%) to ~4x (WalkMe: 88% vs 21% on tool adequacy; Lucidworks: 70% vs 25% on "significant" benefits). Most of these are vendor surveys; the Stanford/NBER and BCG datasets are the most independent.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford/NBER (n>6,000 executives + 3,000 US employees):** executives predict AI will *reduce* employment by 0.7% across all firms (1.2% in US firms); employees predict AI will *increase* employment in their firms by ~0.5% over three years. Executives forecast 2.3% US productivity growth over three years; employees 0.9%. Bloom: "employees know best. Their bosses have drunk the Kool Aid," and "there is a clear misalignment here across society." — [Fortune, Mar 13, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/03/13/ceos-ai-mandate-employees-jobs-survey-nicholas-bloom)
- **Section (5,000 white-collar workers, via WSJ):** >40% of executives say AI saves them >8 hours/week; two-thirds of non-management staff say <2 hours. — [OODA Loop, Jan 22, 2026](https://oodaloop.com/briefs/technology/ceos-say-ai-is-making-work-more-efficient-employees-tell-a-different-story/)
- **Lucidworks 2026 AI Benchmark Study (4th annual; "thousands" of executives and employees, exact n not stated; June 16, 2026, paid placement):** 70% of executive leaders call AI gains "significant" vs. 25% of sub-managerial workers; 91% of self-identified primary decision-makers report large/significant net positive effects vs. 26% of individual contributors not involved in AI decisions; 84% of organizations report measurable AI benefits but only 1% demonstrate advanced AI operationalization; ~89% believe they are keeping pace with or outperforming peers. CEO Mike Sinoway: "Stated satisfaction with AI increases dramatically as you travel up the org chart." Vendor source. — [GlobeNewswire via American Press, June 16, 2026](https://smb.americanpress.com/article/Lucidworks-2026-AI-Benchmark-Study-Reveals-Glaring-Disconnects-Around-AI-Benefits/6a312fe245b635ab6be4e6dd)
- **WalkMe State of Digital Adoption 2026 (3,750 enterprise workers and executives at the same companies, 14 countries):** 88% of executives confident employees have adequate tools vs. 21% of workers; 61% of executives trust AI for complex, business-critical decisions vs. 9% of workers; only 12% of workers fully confident AI tools understand the context of their work. — [WalkMe news release](https://www.walkme.com/news-releases/enterprises-lose-51-workdays-per-employee-to-technology-friction-annually-despite-record-ai-investment-walkme-global-study-of-3750-finds); figures restated in [WalkMe "AI Confidence Trap" release, Aug 25, 2026](https://www.walkme.com/news-releases/the-ai-confidence-trap)
- **WalkMe AI at Work Pulse 2026 (Propeller Insights, 2,037 US adults using AI at work, fielded June 10–20, 2026; Aug 25, 2026):** 53.6% of employees say they have felt a senior leader does not fully understand the AI strategy they are publicly championing; 51% say AI has led their manager/team to expect more output in the same time; 50.2% have spent more time trying to get AI to do a task than doing it manually; 90% feel confident with AI but only 24.6% say it works first try. WalkMe (an SAP company) Global Field CTO KJ Kusch: "Everyone, from the newest hire to the executive suite, is learning AI in real time." — [WalkMe, Aug 25, 2026](https://www.walkme.com/news-releases/the-ai-confidence-trap)
- **People Element 2026 Employee Engagement Report (>94,000 employees):** 76% of executives believe employees are excited about AI; 31% of employees say they are. — via [SBAM/ASE, Kevin Marrs, July 17, 2026](https://www.sbam.org/the-ai-enthusiasm-gap/)
- **SHRM "Navigating AI in the Workplace: 2026" (n not stated):** 80% of senior leaders trust leadership's AI messaging vs. 47% of individual contributors; only one-third of employees said they were informed before AI was implemented. — via [SBAM/ASE, July 17, 2026](https://www.sbam.org/the-ai-enthusiasm-gap/)
- **Perceptyx 2026 Benchmark Report (>23 million employee responses analyzed):** confidence in the organization's future 83% executives vs. 63% individual contributors; only one-third of employees feel well prepared to use AI tools daily; only about half rate change management favorably. — via [SBAM/ASE, July 17, 2026](https://www.sbam.org/the-ai-enthusiasm-gap/)
- **Slingshot Digital Work Trends Report:** 86% of executives say AI use is now effectively required at their companies; only 49% of middle managers say that expectation is communicated to employees. 2026 publication date NOT confirmed. — [Modern Distribution Management](https://www.mdm.com/news/technology/ai/study-86-of-execs-say-they-require-ai-use-at-work-less-than-half-of-managers-agree/)
- **BCG AI Radar 2026:** confidence that AI will pay off falls from 62% among CEOs to 48% among non-tech executives outside the C-suite — BCG attributes this partly to a "change distance" effect. (Figure via WEF write-up by a BCG partner; not on the BCG press release page.) — [World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/stories/leadership/ceos-are-all-in-on-ai-but-anxieties-remain/); [BCG press release, Jan 15, 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/15january2026-as-ai-investments-surge-ceos-take-lead)
- **Gartner CEO–CFO disconnect (330 CEOs, 100 CFOs; Apr 6, 2026):** CEOs' expectations for AI impact outpace CFOs' in speed and scope — a gap *within* the C-suite. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7665661)
- **Mercer Global Talent Trends 2026 (nearly 12,000 executives, HR leaders, investors and employees; fieldwork Sept–Oct 2025; released Feb 25, 2026):** only 32% of executives believe their organizations can optimally combine human and machine capabilities; employee "thriving" fell from 66% in 2024 to 44% in 2026; among 825 C-suite leaders, 99% expect AI to drive at least some headcount reduction within two years. Fieldwork is late 2025 — flag. — [Business Wire, Feb 25, 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260225391406/en)
- **IBM 2026 CEO Study:** CEOs say only 25% of their workforce uses AI regularly, yet 86% believe employees have the skills to collaborate with AI and 83% say AI success depends more on people's adoption than on technology. — [IBM, May 4, 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-05-04-ibm-study-ceos-are-reshaping-c-suite-roles-for-the-ai-era)
- **WRITER × Workplace Intelligence (2,400 knowledge workers incl. 1,200 C-suite and 1,200 employees at gen-AI-using firms; fielded Dec 17, 2025–Jan 25, 2026; Apr 7, 2026):** 97% of executives say AI has been beneficial, yet only 29% see significant ROI from gen AI and 23% from agents; 48% feel AI adoption has been "a massive disappointment"; 75% say their AI strategy is "more for show than for actual internal guidance"; 54% say adopting AI is "tearing their company apart"; 29% of employees (44% of Gen Z) admit to sabotaging their company's AI strategy; 60% of companies plan to lay off employees who can't or won't use AI; 77% of executives say refusers won't be considered for promotion. Vendor-sponsored. — [WRITER press release, Apr 7, 2026](https://writer.com/blog/enterprise-ai-adoption-survey-results-press-release/)

### Inferences
- The gap is directionally robust across independent (NBER), consultancy (BCG) and vendor (WalkMe, Lucidworks, People Element, SHRM, Perceptyx, WRITER) datasets — it is one of the better-replicated findings of 2026 on this topic, even if the magnitudes vary with question wording.
- The gap is *layered*: CEO > other C-suite (Gartner CEO vs CFO; BCG 62% vs 48%; AlixPartners 70% vs 39% on disruption) > VPs < directors (GA/EZRA) > individual contributors. Distance from the decision appears to predict optimism.
- Because executives personally use AI little (Bloom/NBER) while reporting large time savings (Section), the executive perception of benefit is likely formed from delegated reports and dashboards rather than direct experience — which bears on Key Question 3.

### Gaps
- Most gap data are cross-sectional snapshots; only WalkMe (2025→2026 trend on "pretending" questions) offers year-over-year comparison, and it shows the *employee* side improving, not the gap closing.
- No 2026 study measures the gap on *workload* specifically (hours worked, intensity) at the executive level vs. employees; the available "workload" items are hours-saved or output-expectation items.
- Lucidworks did not disclose sample size; SHRM and Slingshot sample sizes not stated in the sources found; Slingshot's 2026 date unconfirmed.

## Key Question 3: Are executives receiving more information (AI summaries, more frequent reporting, agent outputs) or less (because AI filters/synthesizes)? Evidence on executive decision velocity or decision fatigue in 2026

### Takeaway
No 2026 source directly measures the volume of information reaching the C-suite before vs. after AI. The indirect evidence points to *more scrutiny and more decisions* rather than less: boards are asking "far more granular and informed" questions than six months earlier (BCG), 82% of directors rate the AI metrics they receive as fair/poor/not provided (PwC), 72% of executives find it harder to decide what to prioritize (AlixPartners), CEOs personally influence 40+ business-critical decisions a year and 89% of US CEOs say their AI decision involvement has increased (Dataiku). On velocity, CEOs expect AI to make 48% of operational decisions without human intervention by 2030 vs. 25% today (IBM), and 64% are comfortable basing major decisions on AI input — but this is forward-looking self-report, not observed decision speed.

### Cited Findings

**Decision load and involvement**
- Dataiku/Harris Poll (900 CEOs; May 4, 2026): CEOs personally influence >40 business-critical decisions per year; 60% participate in most AI-related decisions; 89% of US CEOs (79% globally) say their involvement in AI decisions has increased; 80% question or challenge AI outputs; 51% require human approval for business-critical decisions (a separate figure says 34% won't let AI decide without human approval — unreconciled in the release); only 25% of CIOs say they can monitor 100% of their agents in real time; only 34% of data leaders are confident their agents could pass a basic decision audit; 51% have delayed AI initiatives due to regulatory uncertainty (up from 37%). — [Dataiku, May 4, 2026](https://www.dataiku.com/en/company/news/global-ai-confessions-report-ceo-edition-2026)
- IBM IBV 2026 CEO Study (2,000 CEOs): 64% comfortable making major strategic decisions based on AI-generated input; CEOs expect 48% of operational decisions ("where consistency and guardrails can be codified") to be made by AI without human intervention by 2030, up from 25% today; 79% of executives confirm they are decentralizing decision-making and distributing accountability as AI plays a larger role. — [IBM, May 4, 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-05-04-ibm-study-ceos-are-reshaping-c-suite-roles-for-the-ai-era)
- AlixPartners (3,200+ executives; Jan 14, 2026): 72% find it increasingly hard to decide which disruptive forces to prioritize (from 67%); more than half of CEOs doubt their teams are agile enough. — [AlixPartners, Jan 14, 2026](https://www.alixpartners.com/newsroom/2026-alixpartners-disruption-index/)
- PwC Global CEO Survey (4,454 CEOs; Jan 2026): CEOs spend almost half their time on decisions with a horizon under one year vs. 16% on five-plus-year decisions. — [People Matters, Jan 20, 2026](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/leadership/global-ceo-confidence-at-five-year-low-as-ai-divides-winners-and-laggards-pwc-survey-shows-at-davos-48108)
- BCG CEO Insomnia Index (≈500 CEOs of companies ≥$100M revenue; fielded Jan 7–21, 2026; published April 2026): 57% say near-term issues "often consume an outsized share of their time"; one in three say they have more to prove to their boards than six months earlier; boards "have been upskilling themselves on geopolitics, tariffs, AI" so "the questions they are posing to CEOs now are far more granular and informed than even six months ago" (Judith Wallenstein, BCG CEO Advisory global head); BCG advises CEOs "Don't outsource your CEO report or board memos" and "Treat decision making as a finite resource" (Christine Barton: "the quality of judgment declines over the course of a day and across a week"). The report explicitly connects CEO stress to "quality decision making": "Chronic stress can narrow a leader's field of vision, reduce cognitive flexibility, and increase risk aversion or impulsivity." — [BCG CEO Insomnia Index PDF, April 2026](https://web-assets.bcg.com/2b/7c/2484f99045e58979f1d4dfd9571d/ceo-insomnia-index-apr-2026.pdf)

**Information quality reaching boards (directors' view of management's AI reporting)**
- PwC 2026 Annual Corporate Directors Survey (~600 US public-company directors; September 2026): 82% rate information linking AI outcomes, risks and business performance as fair, poor or not provided; share rating management's AI information as fair/poor vs. excellent/good — AI strategy and value creation 47% vs. 46%; risk management and controls 50% vs. 43%; workforce readiness for embedding AI 61% vs. 31%; AI investments and ROI 59% vs. 25%; metrics linking AI outcomes, risks and business performance 66% vs. 18%. 71% say their board needs to strengthen AI skills (largest gap vs. cybersecurity 23%, technology 31%); 60% say AI is not yet part of board oversight; most common board use of AI is "staying informed on emerging trends" (28%); 69% name cybersecurity/data privacy/IP as top AI-related concern; recruiting directors for committee leadership "has become harder because of the amount of work and the scrutiny involved." ### Inferences
- The best-supported 2026 reading is that AI has increased the *number and scrutiny* of decisions reaching CEOs (new AI investment, vendor, governance and agent-oversight decisions; more informed boards) while the *quality/synthesis* of AI-related information flowing upward remains poor by directors' own account (PwC: 66% rate outcome-linking metrics fair/poor). That is a "more noise, more asks" pattern rather than an "AI filters it for me" pattern.
- Claims that AI *reduces* executive information load (McKinsey's 1.5M hours; Section's 8+ hours/week) are self-reported by the beneficiaries and have no independent 2026 corroboration.
- IBM's "25% of operational decisions by AI today → 48% by 2030" and Dataiku's 51%-require-human-approval figures imply that in 2026 AI mostly *adds* a review/approval layer at the top rather than removing decisions from it.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study measures volume of board materials, number of dashboards, frequency of reporting, or executive reading/meeting time before vs. after AI adoption.
- No 2026 observed data on executive decision velocity (time-to-decision) was found; all velocity claims are forward-looking expectations.
- No academic 2026 study on executive decision fatigue with AI was retrievable (a 2026 "adaptive cognitive load model" paper surfaced only as a LinkedIn post without abstract).

## Key Question 5: Are executives' hours and stress increasing in 2026 due to AI mandates, shareholder pressure for AI ROI, or faster cycles? CEO turnover/tenure attributed to AI

### Takeaway
2026 executive stress data are substantial and point to high, board-driven strain: CEOs average 66.7/100 on a perceived-stress scale with >70% above the clinical threshold (BCG, n≈500), 45% fear losing their jobs and 40% feel more anxious than a year ago (AlixPartners, n>3,200), 38% report "high or crippling" stress specifically about AI strategy and 64% fear job loss if they fail the AI transition (WRITER, n=1,200 C-suite), 80% say their job is at risk by end-2026 (Dataiku, n=900). But AI itself ranks only 9th of 11 CEO stressors (BCG) — growth targets, costs and board expectations dominate, with AI pressure concentrated at the largest firms (42% of >$5B CEOs feel high urgency to show AI value within six months vs. 28% overall). Turnover evidence is contradictory: 2025 was a record year (168 new S&P 1500 CEOs, tenure down to 8.5 years) and early-2026 press framed an "AI-era CEO churn machine," yet observed H1 2026 counts show global CEO departures at a nine-year low (Russell Reynolds) and Challenger exits down 23% year-to-date through July.

### Inferences
- Stress at the top in 2026 is high and *board/investor-mediated*: the mechanism most sources describe is AI *promises* (investor-day announcements, capex commitments ~1.7% of revenue per BCG) converting into ROI deadlines, rather than AI tools adding to daily hours. BCG's finding that AI is only the 9th stressor overall but that urgency doubles at >$5B firms suggests AI-ROI pressure is a large-cap phenomenon in 2026.
- The CEO-turnover record is best summarized as: *record churn in 2025 and a high-profile cluster of AI-framed exits in Q1 2026, followed by an observed slowdown in H1–July 2026*. Press pieces attributing a 2026 "exodus" to AI rely on 2025 full-year counts and anecdotes; the 2026 counts available as of October do not support an aggregate AI-driven surge. Where AI is cited, it is usually by the departing CEO or board as a reason for *timing* a planned succession (Walmart, Adobe), not as a stated cause of dismissal.
- Executives' own fear of job loss (45–80% depending on survey) is far higher than realized 2026 turnover rates — fear is itself a measured stressor regardless of outcomes.

### Gaps
- No 2026 measurement of executive working hours; "hours" evidence is limited to AI time-saved items.

## Key Question 6: What do 2026 publications say about executives' accountability for AI outcomes, and is this a net addition to their load?

### Takeaway
2026 sources are consistent that AI accountability has migrated to the CEO personally: 72% of CEOs are now the main AI decision maker (BCG, double the prior year); 75% say they own AI decisions (BCG Insomnia); 72% of US CEOs say boards are pressing for measurable AI outcomes, up from 61% (Dataiku); 44% of CEOs struggle to demonstrate AI financial returns (Gartner). Yet only 24% of C-suite leaders say the CEO is formally *accountable* for AI business outcomes (KPMG), and 56% of CEOs report no financial benefit yet (PwC) — a gap between informal ownership and formal accountability that most sources frame as a new, unresolved burden. Where accountability is clearly assigned, reported results are 2.7–3.5x better (KPMG), which 2026 publications use to argue the burden should be embraced, not diffused.

### Inferences
- The 2026 evidence describes a *net addition* to executive load: CEOs took on AI decision ownership (72–75%) and board ROI pressure (72% US) while realized returns remain low (7% established ROI per KPMG; 12% both cost and revenue gains per PwC) — ownership of an unproven asset is the textbook definition of added accountability risk, and it is reflected in the 45–80% of CEOs who say their job is at risk.
- The accountability gap (75% "own" AI vs. 24% formally "accountable") suggests much of the load is informal and therefore not matched by delegated resources or clear metrics — consistent with directors complaining they cannot see AI metrics (PwC) and CEOs being told not to outsource their board memos (BCG).
- Sources that are consultancies or vendors uniformly recommend *more* CEO ownership (KPMG's 3x results, IBM's AI-first C-suite design, BCG's "CEOs take the lead"), so the literature's prescription is to increase, not relieve, executive accountability load.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source quantifies accountability load in hours, number of AI governance committees, or board-meeting AI agenda time.
- No 2026 evidence found on executive compensation plans tying pay to AI outcomes (would be the hardest evidence of formal accountability).
- KPMG's "24% CEO accountable" and Dataiku's "72% board pressure" come from different populations (C-suite/senior leaders vs. CEOs of >$500M firms) and are not directly comparable.
