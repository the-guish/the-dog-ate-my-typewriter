# Information Volume and Flow Inside Organizations in 2026: How Generative/Agentic AI Changed What Circulates, and Who Absorbs It

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers the *information* side of the question (volume of AI-generated text/code/summaries/messages, who receives and reworks it, verification load, after-hours patterns, cognitive load) as distinct from headcount or task workload. Window: publications dated Jan–Oct 2026. Where a 2025 source is the foundational baseline (Microsoft's June 2025 "infinite workday" telemetry, the Sept 2025 HBR/BetterUp/Stanford workslop study, the Sept 2025 ZeroBounce email survey), it is labelled "2025 CONTEXT." Every number below is as reported by the linked source; where I could only reach a secondary summary, that is flagged. Methodology type (telemetry vs. self-report) and level segmentation are noted per item.

Research date: 2026-10-25.

## Key Question 1: What 2026 telemetry or survey data exists on messages, emails, meetings, documents, and interruptions per worker, and how does it compare with 2024–2025 baselines?

### Takeaway
The only large-scale *observed* (telemetry) 2026 dataset I found is ActivTrak's March 2026 State of the Workplace (443M hours, 163,638 employees), which shows that after AI adoption, time in email roughly doubled (+104%) and time in chat more than doubled (+145%), while focus sessions shrank to ~13 minutes and weekend work rose ~50%. Microsoft's May 2026 Work Trend Index exists but its public pages do not refresh the 2025 "infinite workday" per-user message/meeting telemetry; most other 2026 numbers (Atlassian, Gartner, monday.com/Nielsen) are self-report surveys, and none of the telemetry sources segments by hierarchical level.

### Cited Findings

**Observed/telemetry data (2026)**
- ActivTrak Productivity Lab, *2026 State of the Workplace* (5th annual; published March 11, 2026; author Sarah Altemus), analyzed 443M+ hours of behavioral data from 163,638 employees at 1,111 companies across 23+ industries, comparing 2023 with 2025. Methodology: platform telemetry from ActivTrak's own customer base (not a general-population sample). — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/); [PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activtrak-productivity-lab-analyzes-443m-hours-of-work-activity-revealing-ai-is-accelerating-work--not-replacing-it-302710985.html)
- In a 10,584-user subset compared 180 days before vs. after AI adoption, time in email rose +104%, chat/messaging +145%, and business-management apps +94%; "no activity category decreased." ActivTrak's framing: AI is "accelerating work, not replacing it." Not segmented by role or level. — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- Average workday shortened from 8h 53m (2023) to 8h 44m (2025) while average first activity moved earlier from 8:02 a.m. to 7:48 a.m.; productive hours rose 5% to 6h 36m/day; collaboration time +34%; multitasking +12%. — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- Attention fragmentation: average focus session fell 9% to 13m 7s; focus efficiency at 60%, a three-year low. — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- Weekend work: Saturday productive hours rose from 3h 10m to 4h 37m (+46%); Sunday from 2h 30m to 3h 58m (+58%); average Saturday start moved from 8:35 a.m. to 7:11 a.m., Sunday from 12:24 p.m. to 10:58 a.m. ActivTrak: "this isn't occasional overtime, it's become a regular part of the workweek for some." — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- Tool sprawl: AI tools per organization rose from 2 (2023) to 7 (2025); 83% of organizations use 6+ AI tools; 80% of employees use AI tools (up from 53% two years earlier); yet 57% of employees spend <1% of work hours in AI tools and only 3% are in the 7–10% band associated with the highest productivity rate (95%). — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- Burnout risk (ActivTrak's behavioral indicator) fell 22% to 5% of employees, but disengagement risk rose 21% in one year to nearly 1 in 4 employees. — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)

**Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026**
- Microsoft published its sixth annual report, *2026 Work Trend Index Annual Report: Agents, Human Agency, and the Opportunity for Every Organization*, on May 5, 2026. The WorkLab landing page carries only a one-paragraph summary ("As AI and agents take on execution, our own agency expands") and lists the June 17, 2025 "Breaking down the infinite workday" report as a follow-up to the 2025 index. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index)
- Secondary summaries describe the 2026 report as mixed-method: Microsoft 365 telemetry plus a ~20,000-person online survey of AI-using knowledge workers across 10 markets and a separate survey of ~1,800 managers; headline finding that 67% of "AI impact" is associated with organizational factors (culture, managerial support, talent practices) vs. 32% with individual capability; 19% of respondents in a "Frontier" zone; active agents up 15x year-over-year (18x in large enterprises). I could not confirm the sample sizes against the primary report. — [Brian Heger summary](https://www.brianheger.com/2026-work-trend-index-annual-report-agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization-microsoft/); [The Microsoft Cloud Blog evidence check, May 6, 2026](https://themicrosoftcloudblog.com/2026/05/2026-work-trend-index-evidence-check/)

He reads the report as "a strategic argument supported by survey-grade evidence, not as measurement." — [The Microsoft Cloud Blog](https://themicrosoftcloudblog.com/2026/05/2026-work-trend-index-evidence-check/)

**2025 CONTEXT — the Microsoft baseline the 2026 discussion builds on**
- Microsoft's *Breaking Down the Infinite Workday* (June 17, 2025; Microsoft 365 telemetry plus a survey of ~31,000 workers in 31 markets): users interrupted about every two minutes during core hours (secondary sources compute ~275 interruptions/day); 57% of meetings are ad hoc with no calendar invite; 1 in 10 scheduled meetings booked last-minute; nearly a third of meetings span multiple time zones; meetings after 8 p.m. up 16% year-over-year; by 10 p.m. nearly a third of active workers are back in their inboxes; ~20% check email before noon on Saturday and Sunday; 40% of people online at 6 a.m. are reading email; 48% of employees and 52% of leaders say work feels chaotic and fragmented. ### Inferences
- The best observed data (ActivTrak) indicates that AI adoption coincides with *more* time in communication channels, not less: email and chat time roughly doubled for adopters. Because the comparison is 180 days before/after adoption at the individual level, it is more defensible than cross-sectional surveys, but it cannot separate AI's effect from the general trend toward more collaboration (+34% overall).
- Observed behavioral data point to fragmentation (13-minute focus sessions, +12% multitasking, earlier starts, large weekend increases) consistent with — and in some respects worse than — Microsoft's 2025 "infinite workday" telemetry. But no 2026 source re-measured the per-user daily message/meeting/interruption counts Microsoft published in 2025, so year-over-year comparison on those exact metrics is not possible from public sources.
- Tool and agent sprawl (ActivTrak: 2→7 AI tools/org; monday.com: 57% more tools; Microsoft: 15x agents) is itself a plausible source of additional notifications and summaries, but I found no 2026 study that counted notifications per worker.
- None of the telemetry datasets (ActivTrak, Faros, LinearB, Microsoft) publishes a manager/IC/executive split; level-segmented 2026 evidence is almost entirely self-report.

### Gaps
- Whether Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index refreshed the 2025 infinite-workday telemetry (emails/chats per day, interruptions every two minutes, after-8 p.m. meetings) could not be confirmed: the WorkLab landing page and two secondary summaries contained no such numbers, and the full report page was not retrievable.
- No 2026 Slack Workforce Index, Grammarly/Harris Poll State of Business Communication, Asana Work Innovation Lab AI report, Zoom, Owl Labs, Reclaim.ai, Clockwise, RescueTime, Otter, Read AI, or Fellow dataset was found. Asana's most recent AI survey is the 2025 Global State of AI at Work (9,236 knowledge workers, Feb–Aug 2025). — [Asana](https://asana.com/resources/ai-alignment); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/knowledge-workers-say-ai-agents-are-unreliable/801783/)
- No 2026 source quantifies notifications or interruptions per worker, or the number of AI-generated meeting summaries circulating per worker or per level.
- ActivTrak does not disclose how AI users were identified in telemetry.

## Key Question 2: What do 2026 studies say about the cost and distribution of AI-generated low-quality work ("workslop")? Is it flowing upward, downward, or laterally?

### Takeaway
No 2026 follow-up from BetterUp Labs/Stanford was found; the Sept 2025 study (40% received workslop; managers 54% vs ICs 38.5%; ~2 hours per incident; $186/worker/month) remains the anchor that 2026 coverage, Gartner's 2026 trend list, and Microsoft Research's Dec 2025 report all cite. New 2026 surveys point in two directions at once: Founder Reports (Apr 2026, n=2,078) finds the rework burden rises steeply with seniority (ICs 38%, managers 57%, VP/C-suite 63%), implying upward flow, while Zety (Feb/Mar 2026, n=1,000) finds 55% of employees have received workslop from a supervisor, implying substantial downward flow.

### Cited Findings

**2025 CONTEXT — the foundational study**
- BetterUp Labs and Stanford Social Media Lab, *AI-Generated "Workslop" Is Destroying Productivity* (HBR, September 2025; survey of 1,150 US full-time desk workers in September 2025; some summaries cite 1,004): 40% had received workslop in the prior month; respondents estimate 15.4% of the work they receive is workslop; each incident took ~1 hour 56 minutes to resolve; estimated cost $186 per employee per month (~$9M/year for a 10,000-person firm); 53% of recipients annoyed; 42% viewed the sender as less trustworthy; about half judged the sender less creative/capable/reliable; a third were less likely to work with the sender again. — [HBR, Sept 2025](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity); [BetterUp](https://www.betterup.com/blog/hidden-costs-workslop)
- Direction of flow in the 2025 study: 54% of managers vs. 38.5% of individual contributors report receiving workslop; most workslop flows laterally between peers (~40%), with 18% flowing upward to managers and 16% flowing downward from managers. Self-report; segmented by manager vs IC. — [BetterUp](https://www.betterup.com/blog/hidden-costs-workslop); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/what-is-workslop)
- Microsoft Research's *New Future of Work Report 2025* (December 2025) summarizes workslop as AI output that "appears useful but lacks substance, is incomplete, or contains inaccuracies" and "undermines productivity by forcing recipients to interpret, correct, or redo the work" — i.e., the cost shifts to the receiver. — [Microsoft Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/New-Future-Of-Work-Report-2025.pdf)

**2026 surveys and analyses**
- Founder Reports, *2026 AI in the Workplace* (2,078 US workers, Prolific panel, April 2026; published May 27, 2026, author Marc Shorb): 57% of managers have had to fix or redo work from coworkers who relied too heavily on AI vs. 38% of individual contributors; 63% of VPs/C-suite executives have had to fix AI-generated work that didn't meet their standards; 77% review colleagues' AI-assisted work more carefully than fully human work (36% "much more carefully"); 89% have used AI for work, 61% regularly (38% daily, 23% weekly); 44% say their company has no clear AI policy or they are unsure. The author's statement that error-catching "continues to flow upward" is framing, not a measured flow variable; no hours data. Three-level segmentation (IC / manager / VP+C-suite). — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- Zety *Workslop Trust Report* (press release Feb 11, 2026; 1,000 US workers; fieldwork date not stated — one source says January 2025, which conflicts with the 2026 release): 55% have received workslop from a supervisor; 85% say receiving workslop hurts their trust in the sender; 74% say it lowers confidence in the sender's overall work quality; ~45% say it made them more cautious about AI at work; nearly a quarter received no AI training and 45% only limited guidance; two-thirds of employees spend up to six hours a week fixing workslop. No peer/direct-report breakdown. — [Allwork.Space, Mar 18, 2026](https://allwork.space/2026/03/55-of-employees-receive-ai-workslop-from-managers-or-supervisors/); [Zety](https://zety.com/blog/rise-of-workslop-report); [PRNewswire via MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/688486)
- HBR, *AI Adoption Is Overloading Your Middle Managers* (June 26, 2026; Julia Shin, HBS graduate and former Strategy& PwC manager, and Sandra J. Sucher, HBS professor of management practice): based on 18 semi-structured interviews with partners, managers, and junior consultants at two major consulting firms, to understand "how people at each level were actually using AI." Middle managers were "overloaded with new responsibilities — validating AI outputs, identifying errors, coaching their teams in AI skills — all while facing unchanged or even increased delivery pressure and lacking formal support structures," including catching workslop. Qualitative; small sample; consulting only. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers); [Brian Heger summary](https://www.brianheger.com/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers-harvard-business-review/); [CCB Journal](https://ccbjournal.com/blog/ai-productivity-is-creating-manager-overload)
- HBR, *Managers Are Struggling to Keep Up with the AI Productivity Boom* (May 25, 2026; Liz Fosslien, Atlassian marketing leader, and Mollie West Duffy): argues AI lets employees "execute ideas, produce deliverables, and launch projects faster than traditional management systems were designed to handle"; quotes one manager: "Every 30 minutes, someone creates something I have to look at." No methodology or sample; anecdotal. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/05/managers-are-struggling-to-keep-up-with-the-ai-productivity-boom)
- Gartner's nine AI-driven work trends for CHROs in 2026 lists AI "workslop" (fast, poor-quality AI-produced work) as a top productivity drain: pressure to use AI "across many tasks" leaves "limited time to check whether results meet quality expectations," and managers "will need skills to spot disordered AI use." — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/future-of-work-trends); [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/gartner-maps-nine-ai-driven-work-trends-chros-face-by-2026)
- The Next Web (June 20, 2026) restates the 2025 BetterUp/Stanford figures, adds Goldman Sachs (March 2026) finding no meaningful economy-wide link between AI adoption and productivity, and cites an unattributed 2026 survey of 2,400 workers in the US/UK/Europe in which 29% admit actively sabotaging their employer's AI strategy (44% of Gen Z). Publisher of the 2,400-worker survey not named; no manager/IC split. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-workslop-knowledge-decay-harvard-business-review-productivity)
- Inc. (October 2, 2026, Heather Wilde), *AI Created 'Workslop.' Now Managers Have to Deal With It* — commentary rather than new data (page returned 403; characterization from search summary). — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/heather-wilde/ai-created-workslop-now-managers-have-to-deal-with-it/91411699)

### Inferences
- Both 2025 and 2026 self-report data agree that the *receiving* burden rises with seniority (2025: managers 54% vs ICs 38.5%; 2026: 38% → 57% → 63% across IC/manager/VP+). This supports a net upward flow of rework cost, which is consistent with managers' role as reviewers of team output.
- However, the 2025 flow decomposition (40% lateral, 18% up, 16% down) and Zety's 2026 finding that 55% of employees have received workslop from a supervisor indicate the gross flow is substantial in every direction; "who bears the cost" (seniors) is distinct from "where it comes from" (everyone). The report writer should avoid stating that workslop is primarily an IC-to-manager phenomenon.
- The 2026 evidence base on workslop is thinner and lower-quality than the 2025 base: the new sources are vendor/career-site surveys (Zety, Founder Reports) and small qualitative studies (HBR June 2026), not academic replications.

### Gaps
- No 2026 BetterUp Labs or Stanford Social Media Lab follow-up study was found despite targeted searching; no 2026 MIT Sloan Management Review or Forrester workslop study surfaced.
- No 2026 source measures workslop volume as a share of documents/messages received, by level, with telemetry; all figures are self-reported incidence or hours.
- Zety's fieldwork date is inconsistent across sources (Jan 2025 vs. Feb 2026 release); treat its numbers as of uncertain vintage.

## Key Question 3: Who is bearing the verification burden in 2026 — managers reviewing more AI output from teams, or ICs reviewing AI output on behalf of managers?

### Takeaway
The strongest 2026 evidence — from software engineering telemetry (Harvard/Jellyfish 718 firms; CMU-affiliated enterprise study of 196,212 PRs; Faros 22,000 developers; LinearB 8.1M PRs) — shows verification has become the binding constraint: per-reviewer load roughly doubled, median code-review time rose several-fold, and senior engineers absorb the load. Outside engineering, surveys show the review burden landing on managers and executives (monday.com: nearly half of 1,700 directors/senior managers spend more time reviewing team output; Datarails: CFOs spend 26% of their day verifying AI), while ICs are increasingly using AI to pre-verify their *own* upward communications (76% run boss emails through AI) — so both directions are true, with the heavier human-judgment load sitting at senior levels.

### Cited Findings

**Managers and senior leaders as reviewers (2026)**
- monday.com/Nielsen (1,700 directors and senior managers, 7 countries; Oct 2026): nearly half of managers spend more time reviewing and guiding team output than before; more than half of senior leaders say it is harder to evaluate employees' work after AI adoption; 78% have changed how they evaluate employees (weighting prompt-writing, delegation decisions, experimentation); 35% fear being held responsible for decisions made by AI; 46% worry about losing control over agents' actions; 58% expect to oversee more AI agents than human employees within a year; the report calls supervising AI work a new layer of "invisible labor." A search summary attributes a 48% decision-fatigue figure to the same report. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/ai-promises-productivity-but-for-managers-its-mostly-a-headache-for-now-says-new-survey); [monday.com](https://monday.com/blog/project-management/monday-ai-report-2026/)
- Founder Reports (Apr 2026, n=2,078): rework of AI-reliant colleagues' work rises with level — ICs 38%, managers 57%, VP/C-suite 63%; 77% scrutinize AI-assisted work more than human work. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- HBR June 2026 (Shin & Sucher): middle managers in consulting absorb validation, error-spotting, and AI coaching with no relief in delivery pressure. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers)
- HBR/BCG "AI brain fry" study (published March 2026; BCG authors Bedard, Kropp, Hsu, Karaman, Hawes, Keller; survey of ~1,488 / "nearly 1,500" full-time US employees at large companies across industries, roles, and seniority levels): roles requiring sustained monitoring of AI systems produced higher fatigue and information overload; AI expanded workers' "sphere of accountability" — "more output to produce, more outputs to monitor, and more information to manage in the same time"; employees who believed their companies expected more output because of AI reported greater fatigue; managers who made time to answer AI questions reduced their teams' fatigue. Secondary sites report that 14% of AI users (26% in marketing) show "brain fry," with 33% more decision fatigue, 39% more major errors, and 39% higher intent to quit — these specific percentages were not verifiable against the HBR text in this session. Seniority was surveyed but no level-specific results were reported. — [Help Net Security, Mar 9, 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/09/harvard-business-review-ai-workplace-fatigue-report/); [GMU College of Public Health, Mar 24, 2026](https://publichealth.gmu.edu/news/2026-03/ai-and-rise-cognitive-overload); [SVA Consulting (secondary)](https://consulting.sva.com/insights/ai-brain-fry-the-productivity-cost-nobodys-measuring)
- HBR, *The AI Risk Your Company Is Overlooking* (October 6, 2026; Tom Stackpole relaying MIT's Christian Catalini): "when execution is cheap due to AI, verification" becomes the scarce, risky task; successful firms must become "verification factories: institutions capable of properly steering AI-generated output and standing behind the results"; in coding verification can be cheap, but in novel R&D "verification needs to be done by experts, and the risk lies in either overwhelming them or letting outputs go without proper vetting." — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/10/the-ai-risk-your-company-is-overlooking)

**Executives verifying directly (2026)**
- Datarails × Global Surveyz, *2026 CFO Sentiments* (270 US CFOs/finance leaders at firms with 1,000+ employees and $100M+ revenue; fielded July 2026; published September 2026): 96% spend at least 10% of their workday verifying or correcting AI outputs — 56% spend 10–25%, 32% spend 26–50%, 8% spend over 50%; mean 26% of the workday; 76% of teams with data spread across disconnected systems spend >25% of time verifying vs. 31% with centralized data; tasks trusted without review: drafting emails 89%, summarizing meetings 84%; board-ready reports 5%, month-end close 4%; 65% have seen AI give a confident answer based on wrong data; 22% found a fabricated figure; 16% reported an AI error to leadership or the board before catching it. — [Datarails](https://www.datarails.com/research/2026-cfo-sentiments/)
- Workiva, *2026 MidYear Executive Benchmark Survey: The Verification Gap* (finance, audit, and sustainability executives; methodology page returned 403): 84% at least somewhat confident in AI outputs appearing in annual reports without human review; 26% say internal audits caught AI errors that had already reached investors or the board; only 11% say their data quality is sufficient for AI use. — [Workiva](https://www.workiva.com/resources/executive-benchmark-survey-verification-gap)

**Software engineering telemetry (2026) — the clearest "AI produces more than humans can check" evidence**
- Chen & Stratton (Harvard working paper, *Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production*; Jellyfish engineering-analytics data on 718 firms and 725,000+ workers, 2021–2026; reported Oct 11, 2026): AI adoption rose from near zero in late 2024 to >95% by early 2026; code production up 30% (~1,500 lines per worker-month); commits +20%/month; pull requests +23%/month; no statistically significant increase in resolved Jira issues or completed epics; review time +49% (+3.45 days average); PRs requiring revision nearly doubled; comments per review request +35%; 14% more workers pulled into code review; ~80% of firms use AI-assisted review tools but only ~23% of review comments are AI-generated; senior engineers absorb most of the added burden. Caveat: the Forkast article's byline is an "AI mind" (AI-authored); the primary paper was not fetched. — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/ai-coding-agents-write-30-more-code-and-resolve-zero-additional-issues-harvard-study-finds/); [Tech Insider summary](https://tech-insider.org/ai-coding-agents-30-percent-more-code-not-software-2026/)
- He, Agarwal, Denisov-Blanch, Azaletskiy, Koyejo, Vasilescu, *AI Writes Faster Than Humans Can Review: A Longitudinal Study of an Enterprise 2x Mandate* (arXiv 2607.01904, submitted July 2, 2026): 196,212 PRs from 802 developers at a mid-sized "AI-forward" company, Jan 2024–Apr 2026; per-capita merged-PR throughput reached 2.09x the pre-mandate baseline; gains "broadly shared across seniority levels"; code review "restructured around automation: per-reviewer load roughly doubled, automated review overtook human review, and merge and revert rates stayed steady." Staggered difference-in-differences; authors caution adoption was not randomly assigned. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01904)
- Faros AI, *The Acceleration Whiplash* (2026 Engineering Report; telemetry from ~22,000 developers across ~4,000 teams; blog May 21, 2026): comparing high- vs low-AI-adoption teams — median PR review time +441.5%; median time to first review +156.6%; average time in review +199.6%; PRs merged with no review +31.3%; PR size +51.3%; bugs per PR +54%; review comments per PR +25%; daily PR contexts per developer +67.4%; 25% of PRs now reviewed by AI agents (0% in 2025); senior engineers are "disproportionately absorbing this load." — [Faros AI](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-code-quality-senior-engineer-review-burden); [FlowVerify summary, Aug 3, 2026](https://www.flowverify.co/blog/ai-code-review-bottleneck-2026-data)
- LinearB *2026 Software Engineering Benchmarks* (8.1M PRs, 4,800 teams, 42 countries): AI-assisted PRs are 400+ lines at the 75th percentile vs 157 for unassisted; reviewer pickup time ~16+ hours (17.6h) vs ~200 minutes; 30-day merge rate 32.7% vs 84.5%. — [LinearB](https://linearb.io/blog/8-million-prs-engineering-productivity); [LinearB library](https://linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development)
- GitHub engineering telemetry (60M+ automated code reviews; date not confirmed): roughly one in five reviews now involves an agent; agent-authored PRs carry more technical debt yet human reviewers approve them more readily. — [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/agent-pull-requests-are-everywhere-heres-how-to-review-them/)
- Qodo *2026 State of AI Code Quality* (500 US developers + 300 engineering leaders): reviewing/validating AI-generated code is the top delivery bottleneck for both groups (26% each); 89% of organizations have had an AI-related production incident. — [Qodo](https://www.qodo.ai/blog/state-of-ai-code-quality-report-2026/)
- Sonar *State of Code* (January 2026; 1,100+ developers): 96% do not fully trust AI-generated code; only 48% always verify before committing; 38% say reviewing AI code takes more effort than reviewing human colleagues' code. — [Sonar](https://www.sonarsource.com/company/press-releases/sonar-data-reveals-critical-verification-gap-in-ai-coding/)

**ICs pre-verifying their own upward communication (2026)**
- Omni Calculator *AI Second Opinion Survey* (2026; 705 employed US adults who use AI for writing; Prolific; chi-square at 95%): 76% run emails to their boss through AI before sending; 58% do so for Slack messages to their boss; 77% for meeting notes; 77% have delayed sending something to get AI's review; 79% have rewritten something on AI's input even when they thought it was fine. High-frequency checkers (70%+ of writing): managers 54%, executives 48%, entry-level ICs 42%, experienced ICs 30%; 32% feel less confident in their own writing since using AI (Gen Z 42%). — [Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/reports/ai-second-opinion-survey)

**Other 2026 review-burden data points**
- Fullstory survey: 80% of digital-experience professionals say AI has increased their review workload (date not confirmed as 2026). — [Fullstory](https://www.fullstory.com/blog/ai-product-development-survey/)
- Enhesa 2026 benchmark: 40% of compliance teams act on AI-generated regulatory guidance "always or often" without expert review. — [EHS Today](https://www.ehstoday.com/safety-leadership/news/55413062/skipping-ai-review-of-compliance-results-in-higher-fines)
- Gartner survey of 533 CIOs/senior IT leaders: only 40% have a strategy to stop AI use eroding employee capability; Gartner predicts 30% of organizations will find AI has weakened independent judgment in at least one core role by 2030 (date not confirmed as 2026). — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/gartner-only-40-of-cios-guard-against-ai-dulling-staff-judgment)

### Inferences
- The engineering telemetry is the only place where the "AI produces more than humans can check" dynamic is directly observed: output (PRs, commits, lines) rose 20–100%+, finished work did not rise proportionally, and review latency and per-reviewer load roughly doubled. The response pattern — automated review overtaking human review (He et al.), 25% of PRs AI-reviewed (Faros), 31% more PRs merged with no review — suggests verification is being partly abandoned or delegated to AI rather than fully absorbed by humans.
- Across domains, the human verification residue lands at senior levels: senior engineers (code), middle managers (consulting), directors/senior managers (monday.com), CFOs (finance). ICs' verification activity in 2026 is mostly *self*-verification of their own AI-assisted output before sending it up, which reduces the load on managers only if it works.
- The Datarails trust pattern (89% trust AI-drafted emails unreviewed; 5% trust board reports) implies the verification burden concentrates on high-stakes, upward-facing documents — exactly the material executives and managers consume.

### Gaps
- No 2026 cross-industry study directly compares hours spent verifying AI output by level (IC vs first-line vs middle vs executive). Founder Reports gives incidence, not hours; Datarails gives hours but only for CFOs.
- The HBR/BCG "brain fry" seniority results were collected but not published in the sources available; the widely circulated 14%/33%/39% figures are from secondary sites.
- Engineering telemetry reports (Faros, LinearB, Jellyfish/Harvard) do not separate first-line engineering managers from senior ICs when describing who "absorbs" review.

## Key Question 4: Do executives experience less information overload in 2026 because of AI summarization and "chief of staff" agents, or more because of faster reporting cycles?

### Takeaway
I found no 2026 evidence that AI summarization or chief-of-staff agents measurably reduced executive information load; the "AI chief of staff" literature is entirely vendor content. The 2026 survey evidence points the other way: executives report the highest rework rates (63%), CFOs spend a quarter of the day verifying AI, more than half of senior leaders find it harder to evaluate work, and 26% of finance executives say AI errors reached boards or investors before being caught.

### Cited Findings
- Founder Reports (Apr 2026): 63% of VPs and C-suite executives have had to fix AI-generated work that didn't meet their standards — higher than managers (57%) or ICs (38%). — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- Datarails (Sept 2026): CFOs average 26% of their workday verifying/correcting AI; 16% reported an AI error to leadership or the board before catching it; only 5% would trust AI alone for board-ready reports. — [Datarails](https://www.datarails.com/research/2026-cfo-sentiments/)
- Workiva (mid-2026): 26% of finance/audit/sustainability executives say internal audits caught AI errors that had already reached investors or the board, while 84% are at least somewhat confident in unreviewed AI output — a confidence/verification mismatch at the top. — [Workiva](https://www.workiva.com/resources/executive-benchmark-survey-verification-gap)
- monday.com/Nielsen (Oct 2026; 1,700 directors/senior managers): more than half of senior leaders say AI makes it harder to evaluate employees' work; 46% use AI to inform decisions; 39% use AI to draft performance reviews; 58% expect to oversee more AI agents than humans within a year; a 48% decision-fatigue figure is attributed to the report by a search summary. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/ai-promises-productivity-but-for-managers-its-mostly-a-headache-for-now-says-new-survey); [monday.com](https://monday.com/blog/project-management/monday-ai-report-2026/)
- Atlassian (Apr 2026; 173 Fortune 1000 executives): 89% say AI increases speed; only 6% are sure they have clear organization-wide ROI; 55% say AI is widening gaps between teams; 70% expect more collaborative roles and 77% more horizontal teams with fewer layers. — [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/state-of-teams-2026)
- Gartner (Oct 20, 2026): 44% of CEOs struggle to demonstrate AI financial returns. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-says-44-percent-of-ceos-struggle-to-demonstrate-ai-financial-returns-as-technology-becomes-central-to-growth)
- Grant Thornton *2026 AI Impact Survey* (950 C-suite and senior leaders): 78% lack strong confidence they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days. — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/services/advisory-services/artificial-intelligence/2026-ai-impact-survey)
- Deloitte Human Capital Trends 2026 (secondary summary only): 60% of executives use AI in decision-making but only 5% say they manage it well; 73% of organizations are redesigning work around AI. Not verified against Deloitte's report. — [Mindset Online](https://mindsetonline.com/deloitte-human-capital-2026-73-percent-redesigning-work-ai-not-told-employees/)
- Omni Calculator (2026): executives are the group most likely to say AI changed their sending behavior (81%, vs managers 78%); 48% of executives check 70%+ of their writing with AI; executives have the lowest "delay to get AI review" rate (70%) of any corporate role. — [Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/reports/ai-second-opinion-survey)
- 2025 CONTEXT: Microsoft (June 2025) found 52% of leaders vs 48% of employees say work feels chaotic and fragmented — leaders slightly *more* overloaded than employees before the 2026 agent wave. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday)
- 2025 CONTEXT: Slack's seniority gradient (daily AI use: executives 43%, senior managers 35%, middle managers 23%, ICs 10%) shows executives were the heaviest personal users of AI tools, which is the precondition for AI filtering to help them. — [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/daily-ai-workforce-use-growth/)
- "AI chief of staff" agents: 2026 coverage consists of vendor blogs (ISHIR, get-alfred.ai) and an SEO content site (withtai.com) whose specific statistics (e.g., a claimed Harvard finding of 22% higher strategic-focus scores; "40% of executives disabled proactive features due to privacy concerns") could not be verified and reference product names I could not confirm exist. Asana launched an "AI chief of staff" feature in 2025. These sources describe the *intended* benefit (triage email/calendar, surface only decisions needing a human) and the failure modes (over-escalation, unauthorized actions, missed nuance in summaries) but provide no measured reduction in executive information load. — [ISHIR](https://www.ishir.com/blog/326480/ai-chief-of-staff-how-agentic-ai-helps-ceos-run-faster-more-aligned-teams.htm); [withtai.com (unverified)](https://withtai.com/knowledge/how_do_executives_implement_agentic_ai_for_personal_productivity_and_chief-of-staff_duties_in_2026.php)

### Inferences
- The available 2026 data describe executives as *consumers of unverified AI output with high confidence* (Workiva 84%) who nonetheless discover errors after the fact (26%) and spend substantial time verifying (Datarails 26% of day). That is consistent with faster, higher-volume reporting cycles raising executive load, not with AI filtering lowering it.
- The asymmetry the question hypothesizes (summarization helps those who receive; AI increases volume for those who produce) is partly visible: Datarails CFOs trust AI meeting summaries (84%) and drafted emails (89%) without review, so low-stakes summaries probably do reduce reading load — but the same executives refuse to trust AI for board reports (5%), so the high-stakes verification load remains theirs.
- Because 2026 executive surveys are largely vendor-sponsored (Datarails, Workiva, monday.com, Grant Thornton), the "more overload" conclusion is directional, not quantified against a 2024–2025 baseline.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study measures executive information load (messages, reports, dashboards received per week) before vs after adopting AI summarization or agents.
- No independent (academic or non-vendor) 2026 evidence on AI chief-of-staff agents' effect on executive attention was found.
- No 2026 data on whether reporting cadence (weekly → daily dashboards, auto-generated board packs) has accelerated because of AI.

## Key Question 6: Are there 2026 studies on cognitive load, attention fragmentation, or "AI fatigue" segmented by role or level?

### Takeaway
The HBR/BCG "AI brain fry" study (Mar 2026, ~1,500 US employees) is the main 2026 study of AI-related cognitive load and ties fatigue to *oversight* of multiple AI systems and to expectations of more output — but it segments by function (marketing highest, legal lowest), not by hierarchical level. Level-relevant 2026 data are indirect: Gartner's 47% of managers working harder, monday.com's decision fatigue among directors/senior managers, Founder Reports' seniority-graded rework rates, and ActivTrak's population-wide 13-minute focus sessions.

### Cited Findings
- HBR/BCG (Mar 2026; Bedard et al.; ~1,488 US full-time employees at large companies): "AI brain fry" defined as "mental fatigue from excessive use or oversight of AI tools beyond a person's cognitive capacity"; symptoms: mental fog, headaches, slower decisions, "crowded thinking"; especially tied to managing multiple AI systems at once; prevalence highest in marketing, high in people ops, operations, engineering, finance, IT, lowest in legal; a small set of AI tools aligned with productivity gains and adding more tools reduced gains; using AI to remove routine tasks lowered burnout; team-level integration produced less strain than individual adoption. Seniority was in the sample but no level results were published in accessible sources. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/09/harvard-business-review-ai-workplace-fatigue-report/); [GMU](https://publichealth.gmu.edu/news/2026-03/ai-and-rise-cognitive-overload)
- GMU College of Public Health commentary (Mar 24, 2026; Dean Melissa J. Perry, originally in Psychology Today): no original data; frames AI as generating "endless variations of work" on top of accumulating email and messages, and ties constant notifications/AI outputs to novelty-seeking attention effects (citing Ophir, Nass & Wagner 2009 on media multitaskers). — [GMU](https://publichealth.gmu.edu/news/2026-03/ai-and-rise-cognitive-overload)
- ActivTrak (Mar 2026, telemetry): average focus session 13m 7s (-9% since 2023); focus efficiency 60% (three-year low); multitasking +12%; collaboration +34%; disengagement risk up 21% to ~1 in 4; burnout-risk indicator down 22% to 5%. Not segmented by level. — [ActivTrak](https://www.activtrak.com/blog/2026-state-of-the-workplace/)
- monday.com/Nielsen (Oct 2026; 1,700 directors/senior managers): 48% report decision fatigue from choosing where to apply AI (per search summary); 35% fear liability for AI decisions; 46% worry about losing control of agents. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/ai-promises-productivity-but-for-managers-its-mostly-a-headache-for-now-says-new-survey)
- Gartner (Apr 29, 2026; 2,947 employees/managers): 47% of managers say more is expected of them and they work harder than a year ago; 62% feel obligated to protect their teams; 45% have made decisions putting employee interests ahead of the business. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-29-gartner-hr-survey-finds-47-percent-of-managers-say-they-are-working-harder-than-1-year-ago)
- Founder Reports (Apr 2026): rework burden by level — ICs 38%, managers 57%, VP/C-suite 63%. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- HBR June 2026 (Shin & Sucher): middle managers in consulting describe overload from validating AI outputs and coaching on top of unchanged delivery pressure. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers)
- Omni Calculator (2026): 32% feel less confident in their own writing since using AI (Gen Z 42%, Millennials 33%, Gen X 25%, Boomers 21%); "Reassurance-Driven" reliance highest among entry-level ICs (21%) and executives (21%), lowest among experienced ICs (12%). — [Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/reports/ai-second-opinion-survey)
- Gartner (533 CIOs; date unconfirmed): only 40% have a strategy to prevent AI dulling employee judgment; 30% of organizations predicted to find weakened independent judgment in a core role by 2030. — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/gartner-only-40-of-cios-guard-against-ai-dulling-staff-judgment)
- 2025 CONTEXT: Microsoft (June 2025) — 48% of employees and 52% of leaders say work feels chaotic and fragmented. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday)
- Unverified secondary claims to avoid citing as fact: "77% of managers believe AI improves efficiency while 88% of heavy AI users report increased burnout" (attribution inconsistent across sources); a Google Cloud figure that frequent AI users are 45% more likely to experience high burnout (no primary source found); a clearing-ai.com "AI fatigue by career stage" page that labels its own numbers synthesized estimates. — [Medium (secondary)](https://medium.com/@TheDailyDrift/ai-was-supposed-to-give-you-your-time-back-instead-its-burning-you-out-7ce7339c28f7); [clearing-ai.com (synthesized estimates)](https://clearing-ai.com/stats.html)

### Inferences
- The 2026 cognitive-load literature locates fatigue in *oversight* (monitoring many AI systems, verifying output) and in *expectation inflation* (more output expected because AI exists) rather than in AI use per se. Since oversight concentrates at manager and senior-IC levels (Q3), the mechanism implies higher AI-related cognitive load at those levels — but this is inference; the BCG study did not publish a level split.
- ActivTrak's telemetry suggests attention fragmentation is a population-wide phenomenon in 2025 data, not level-specific, and its burnout indicator actually improved — a tension with survey-based "AI fatigue" narratives that the report writer should note (behavioral burnout proxies vs self-reported fatigue measure different things).
- Entry-level ICs show a distinct load pattern: not rework of others' output, but confidence erosion and heavy self-verification (Omni Calculator), consistent with AI shifting junior workers from producing to checking.
