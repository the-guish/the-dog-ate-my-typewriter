# First-Line and Middle Managers Under Generative/Agentic AI: What 2026 Publications Say

Scope: publications dated January–October 2026 on the middle of the management pyramid (first-line managers / team leads / supervisors, and middle managers / managers-of-managers / directors). Items from 2024–2025 are included only where a 2026 source leans on them and are explicitly labelled "(2025 context)" or "(2024 context)". Where a 2026 date could not be confirmed, the item is flagged. Research conducted 2026-10-25.

Source-quality legend used throughout: [primary] = publisher's own report/release; [secondary] = journalism or summary of a primary; [vendor] = vendor-commissioned survey; [self-report] = survey of perceptions rather than observed/administrative data.

## Key Question 1: Middle-manager headcount, span of control, and delayering in 2026 — did "the great flattening" continue or reverse?

### Takeaway
Delayering clearly continued through 2026: Korn Ferry finds 42% of organizations cut management roles in the past year, Gallup's average U.S. span of control rose from 10.9 to 12.1 direct reports, and Amazon (16,000 cuts, Jan 2026), Meta (~8,000, May 2026), Uber (managers -20%, Sept 2026), Cloudflare, PayPal, Block, Coinbase and GitLab all framed 2026 cuts around "layers." The first credible sign of partial reversal appeared in September 2026, when Meta began asking individual contributors to return to manager roles; no systematic 2026 dataset yet shows manager headcount recovering, and Gartner's much-repeated 2024 forecast (20% of firms eliminating >50% of middle-management roles by 2026) has not been validated by any 2026 measurement found.

### Cited Findings

**Survey and aggregate measures of delayering (2026)**

- Gallup, "Span of Control: What's the Optimal Team Size for Managers?" **Academic evidence on hierarchy and span (2026)**
- Shan & Zhu, "AI Exposure and Organizational Structure" (SSRN, March 2026; 7,681 U.S. firms, 1990–2020, difference-in-differences) [primary, observed data but pre-generative-AI period]: "Firms exposed to AI reduce the number of hierarchical ranks while simultaneously increasing managerial spans of control," with effects stronger in technical firms and high-automation industries. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6456498) (abstract only; page returned 403, confirmed via search)
- Baumann & Wu, "The Agentic Organization: From Span of Control to Span of Evaluation" (SSRN, posted 18 Oct 2026; Univ. of Southern Denmark / Michigan Ross) [primary, theory]: "Agentic AI may loosen this constraint by taking over both execution and evaluation, allowing firms to expand without comparable increases in employees or supervisors," but the human review load ("span of evaluation") can rise even when reporting lines are unchanged. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7630858) (abstract only)
- Friebel et al., "Pyramids, Diamonds, and Oscillations" (dated April 2026; model of internal labor markets) [theory]: for learning shocks "the long-run span falls, potentially permanently shifting the firm from a pyramid to a diamond." Venue/NBER status unconfirmed (hosted on an author's site). — [PDF](https://soumitrashukla.github.io/Pyramids_Diamonds_Oscillations_NBERORG_2026.pdf)
- Xu et al., "Generative AI and Organizational Structure in the Knowledge Economy" (arXiv 2506.00532; first posted 2025, revision date unconfirmed) [theory]: span of control is non-monotonic — "contracts initially and expands later as GenAI advances." Contradicts the simple "flatter" story. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00532)

### Inferences
- The 2026 evidence is directionally consistent (fewer layers, wider average spans, management roles cut at ~4 in 10 organizations), but the *median* U.S. span is unchanged at ~6; the average rose because a small share of teams became very large (25+). Flattening in 2026 is concentrated, not universal.
- Company rationales in 2026 (Amazon, Uber, Cloudflare, Block, Coinbase) emphasize bureaucracy, "measurers," micro-teams and fragmented ownership rather than AI replacing managerial work directly; AI is more often the budget justification (capex reallocation) than the mechanism.
- Meta's Sept 2026 re-creation of manager roles is the first high-profile reversal and coincides with Korn Ferry's finding that 39% of workers feel directionless without managers; it is one company and one division, so it is a leading indicator, not a trend.
- The empirical paper most cited for "AI flattens hierarchies" (Shan & Zhu) uses data ending in 2020, i.e. pre-ChatGPT; the 2026 theory papers (Baumann & Wu; Xu et al.) argue the effect on spans is contingent or non-monotonic.

### Gaps
- No 2026 administrative/observed dataset (Live Data, Revelio, Gusto, BLS OES) with a manager-specific headcount or span series was retrievable; Live Data's June 2026 "purge" piece and the WSJ Sept 2026 exit-share figure are behind paywalls or missing pages.
- Nothing was found for Bayer, Intel or UPS in 2026 that specifically quantifies manager cuts; UPS figures (30,000 operations jobs in 2026 vs a 12,000 figure) conflict across sources and are not manager-specific.
- No 2026 Gartner measurement of actual AI-driven delayering was found; the "20% / >50%" figure remains a 2024 forecast.
- Gartner's "Organization Design Benchmarks: Manager Spans of Control, Management Layers and Responsibilities" exists but is paywalled; date and figures unconfirmed. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6332079)

## Key Question 2: Are first-line managers reporting higher workload and information volume because AI lets ICs produce more that must be reviewed? 2026 data on hours, meetings, "verification burden"

### Takeaway
Every 2026 survey that asked found a verification burden: 47% of 1,701 directors/senior managers (monday.com/Nielsen, Oct 2026) spend more time reviewing and guiding team output, 57% of managers vs 38% of ICs have had to fix or redo colleagues' over-AI-reliant work (Founder Reports, May 2026), and HBR's June 2026 interview study found middle managers absorbing "validating AI outputs, identifying errors, coaching their teams in AI skills" with unchanged or higher delivery pressure. Hard numbers on hours and meeting counts are scarce; the only time-based figures are Gartner's ~9 hours/week on employees' personal/emotional issues and Australia-specific reports of longer working days.

### Cited Findings
- monday.com "AI Report 2026" (research by Nielsen; 1,701 directors and senior managers in seven countries; published 21 Oct 2026) [vendor][self-report]: 47% "spend more time reviewing and guiding team output"; in Australia 59%, where "a third report longer working days as a result"; 78% changed how they evaluate team members since adopting AI; 53% say AI "has made it harder to evaluate original contributions"; 46% report greater accountability for AI-generated outputs; 45% face increased pressure to redesign workflows; about one-third do not agree they have received sufficient AI training; 57% say more tools are in circulation and 51% say tools are more interdependent; only 29% describe AI infrastructure as fully optimized and centralized. — [monday.com](https://monday.com/blog/project-management/monday-ai-report-2026/); TechCrunch coverage (Ram Iyer, 21 Oct 2026): "AI promises productivity, but for managers, it's mostly a headache for now"; the report describes an unquantified "invisible labor" of supervising AI work. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/ai-promises-productivity-but-for-managers-its-mostly-a-headache-for-now-says-new-survey/)
- Founder Reports, "AI Is Quietly Adding to Your Manager's Workload" (27 May 2026; 2,078 U.S. workers via Prolific, April 2026) [self-report]: "57% of managers have had to fix or redo work from coworkers who relied too heavily on AI, compared with 38% of individual contributors"; 63% of VPs/C-suite have had to fix AI-generated work; 77% of all respondents review AI-assisted work more carefully than fully human work, 36% "much more carefully." — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- HBR, "AI Adoption Is Overloading Your Middle Managers" (Julia Shin & Sandra J. Sucher, 26 June 2026; 18 semi-structured interviews with partners, managers and junior consultants at two major consulting firms) [primary, qualitative]: senior leaders were "expanding scope, accelerating delivery with leaner teams, and reimagining services"; junior consultants reported large productivity gains; middle managers took on "validating AI outputs, identifying errors, coaching their teams in AI skills" while "delivery pressure stayed the same or rose" and they "lacked formal support" — "the middle layer is failing under this load." — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers); [HBR Store description](https://store.hbr.org/product/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers/H0981H)
- HBR, "AI Is Squeezing Middle Managers" (Gretchen Gavett, 30 June 2026) [secondary on the above]: managers are "drowning from being responsible for catching 'workslop'" (AI-generated content that looks polished but lacks substance) while "the ranks of middle managers have been thinning due to the rise of flatter organizational structures." — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-is-squeezing-middle-managers) (paywalled; via search excerpt)
- Gartner HR survey (press release 22 May 2026; 2,947 employees and managers, fielded Nov–Dec 2025) [primary][self-report]: "47% of managers say that more is expected of them, and they must work harder today compared to one year ago"; 66% say their primary responsibility is managing the people on their teams above driving progress toward organizational goals; 72% say delivering a positive employee experience is their primary responsibility; the average manager spends "nearly one-quarter of their time" (about nine hours a week) engaging with employees on personal and emotional issues (Tony Guadagni, Gartner); 62% feel obligated to protect their teams. The release does not isolate AI-driven review work. — [BizTech Reports summary of Gartner release](https://www.biztechreports.com/news-archive/2026/5/14/gartner-hr-survey-finds-47-of-managers-say-they-are-working-harder-than-one-year-ago-gartner-may-18-2026) (secondary; release dates cited inconsistently as 14, 18 and 22 May 2026)
- Korn Ferry Workforce 2026 (8 Sept 2026; >16,000) [primary][self-report]: 52% of "AI-weary" workers say AI "has increased their workloads"; 62% say workloads increased significantly over two years; 61% perform the responsibilities of more than one role (the "two-job job"); 45% are "too busy to deliver meaningful results"; 63% say AI improved their efficiency while 52% say AI tools raised the number of tasks expected of them; 49% are worn out by the pace of change. — [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/about-us/press/korn-ferry-workforce-2026-report-unlocking-growth-requires-rethinking-how-work-gets-done); [Journal of Accountancy, 15 Sept 2026, Bryan Strickland](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2026/sep/driving-efficiency-or-driving-workers-toward-burnout-how-ai-is-being-used/)
- Gallup span-of-control study (13 Jan 2026) [primary]: 97% of U.S. managers have IC responsibilities ("player-coaches"); median 40% of time on IC work; among managers above 40% IC time, nearly half manage <5 people and about three-fourths <10; only 16% of ~15,000 employees said their last manager conversation was "extremely meaningful." — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/700718/span-control-optimal-team-size-managers.aspx)
- McKinsey State of AI 2026 (Aug 2026) [primary][self-report]: 47% of mid-level managers and ICs report at least one negative effect of AI (e.g., pressure to take on more work, being overwhelmed by AI output) vs 31% of executives and senior managers; a secondary breakdown reports 20% of midlevel managers say AI hurts their ability to think critically vs 9% of C-level. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) (via search confirmation; direct fetch 503); [k-gsp.org summary](https://www.k-gsp.org/p/the-coming-polarization-what-mckinseys)
- Workiva 2026 MidYear Executive Benchmark Survey (finance/audit executives) [vendor]: 26% said internal audits caught AI errors that reached external audiences or board members, yet 84% were at least somewhat confident in AI output accuracy without human review. — [Workiva](https://www.workiva.com/resources/executive-benchmark-survey-verification-gap)
- Robert Half (10 Mar 2026; HR leaders) [vendor]: 84% of HR teams feel overworked by the added time needed to review AI-generated candidate applications; 67% say AI-generated applications are slowing hiring. — [Robert Half](https://press.roberthalf.com/2026-03-10-Robert-Half-survey-67-of-HR-leaders-report-AI-generated-applications-are-slowing-hiring)
- Baumann & Wu (SSRN, 18 Oct 2026) [theory]: formalizes the above as "span of evaluation" — "how much review work an accountable person or team can absorb, given their expertise, authority, and attention"; "adding an activity can force a firm to restrict delegation elsewhere, even when AI performance is unchanged." — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7630858)

### Inferences
- The verification burden is now documented by four independent 2026 surveys plus a qualitative HBR study, all pointing the same way; the mechanism (ICs generate more polished output faster; managers remain accountable for quality) is consistent with Baumann & Wu's "span of evaluation" concept.
- The burden appears to fall on whoever is the first accountable reviewer, which for IC output is the first-line manager; the HBR consulting study shows middle managers (engagement managers) bearing it where juniors use AI heavily.
- Most "workload up" figures are perceptions; the only time-denominated figures (Gartner ~9 hrs/week on personal issues; Australia "a third report longer days") are not AI-specific.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source gives managers' weekly hours, meeting counts, or number of dashboards/escalations before vs after AI adoption; Microsoft's WTI 2026 contains no meeting-load or workload telemetry for managers (see Q5).
- No source quantified the number of AI-generated reports or summaries reaching managers ("information volume") directly; the closest proxies are tool sprawl (57% more tools) and the 47%/57% review figures.
- The full HBR Shin & Sucher text (remedies, failure modes) is paywalled; findings above come from the HBR summary and store description.

## Key Question 3: Is manager engagement/burnout worse or better in 2026 than 2025?

### Takeaway
Worse. Gallup's SOGW 2026 shows global manager engagement at 22% for 2025 (from 27% in 2024 and 31% in 2022), the largest single-year fall on record, while IC engagement was roughly flat (19%); Korn Ferry finds 55% of remaining managers "exhausted"; WebMD Health Services' 2026 survey (n=3,872) finds middle managers carry the heaviest burnout burden and the most productive AI users have 4.5x the burnout risk. No 2026 source shows manager engagement improving.

### Cited Findings
- Gallup State of the Global Workplace 2026 (April 2026; 2025 data, 263,810 respondents, 141,444 employed; margin of error on global engagement <±0.1 pt) [primary][self-report]: global manager engagement 31% (2022) → 27% (2024) → 22% (2025); "the largest year-over-year drop in manager engagement occurred between 2024 and 2025, when it declined by five points from 27% to 22%"; IC engagement 20% (2022) → 19% (2025). U.S. managers 36% engaged in 2025. In best-practice organizations 79% of managers were engaged ("nearly four times the global average"). South Asia managers -8 points in 2025. — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/349484/state-of-the-global-workplace.aspx)
- Same report, leader wellbeing [primary]: leaders (middle-to-upper) 26% engaged, 43% thriving; daily stress 46%, anger 33%, sadness 34%, loneliness 31%; versus ICs leaders were 7 pts more likely to report stress, 12 pts anger, 11 pts sadness, 10 pts loneliness. Engaged managers/leaders are 14 pts more likely to be thriving than the average leader. The report does not publish a burnout metric. — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/349484/state-of-the-global-workplace.aspx)
- Gallup span-of-control study (13 Jan 2026) [primary]: U.S. manager engagement average cited as 37%; managers spending <40% of time on IC work "tend to maintain higher engagement than the average (37%), regardless of the number of workers reporting to them," while those above 40% have lower engagement that "worsens as team size grows." — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/700718/span-control-optimal-team-size-managers.aspx)
- Korn Ferry Workforce 2026 (8 Sept 2026) [primary][self-report]: "more than half (55%) of the managers who remain say they are exhausted"; employee motivation fell from 71% (2024) to 61% (2026). — [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/about-us/press/korn-ferry-workforce-2026-report-unlocking-growth-requires-rethinking-how-work-gets-done)
- WebMD Health Services, 2026 Workplace and Employee Survey (June 2026; 3,872 full-time U.S. employees) [vendor][self-report]: "the most productive AI users carry a burnout risk 4.5 times higher than their peers"; "middle managers carry the heaviest burnout burden of all," reported in coverage as more than three times the burnout of individual contributors; workers reporting low well-being rose nearly 40% over two years. — [WebMD Health Services report PDF](https://www.webmdhealthservices.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/WebMD-Health-Services-2026-Workplace-and-Employee-Survey-Report.pdf) (too large to fetch; figures via [Employee Benefit News, Jimmy Nesbitt, 8 July 2026](https://www.benefitnews.com/news/ai-burnout-is-surging-at-work) and [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91621755/with-ai-productivity-your-team-is-burning-out) (403), so the ">3x" middle-manager figure is unverified against the primary)
- (2025 context) WebMD Health Services' 2024 survey: 53% of managers vs 33% of employees reported burnout; managers 24% more likely to consider quitting within six months (meQuilibrium). — [WebMD Health Services blog, 1 Mar 2025](https://www.webmdhealthservices.com/blog/manager-burnout-is-getting-worse-strategies-to-help-managers-cope/)

## Key Question 4: What do 2026 Gartner/McKinsey/BCG/Deloitte/HBR/MIT Sloan pieces say about AI absorbing managerial tasks and what fills the freed time?

## Key Question 6: Are first-line managers and middle managers affected differently?

### Takeaway
Sources rarely separate the two tiers, but where they do, the pattern is: middle managers (managers-of-managers, directors, "measurers," micro-team bosses) are the primary *headcount* target in 2026 cuts (Meta, Uber, Cloudflare, Amazon's "layers"), while first-line/player-coach managers bear the *workload* shock — 97% hold IC duties with a median 40% of time on IC work, their engagement falls fastest when IC load exceeds 40%, they are the first reviewers of AI-assisted output, and frontline leaders are the least equipped for change (15% strong at facilitating change vs 30% of mid-level). McKinsey's 47%-vs-31% negative-effect gap groups mid-level managers with ICs against executives/senior managers, placing both lower tiers on the losing side.

### Cited Findings
- Gallup (13 Jan 2026) [primary]: 97% of U.S. managers have IC responsibilities ("player-coaches"); median 40% of time on IC work; managers above 40% IC time are concentrated in small teams (nearly half manage <5, about three-fourths <10) — i.e., first-line supervisors; their engagement is below the 37% average and worsens as teams grow. Meta-analysis (312 studies, 92,252 teams, 897,971 employees): engagement–productivity links are strongest in small (4–9) and medium (10–19) teams; in 20+ teams the productivity/turnover link "varies by industry." — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/700718/span-control-optimal-team-size-managers.aspx)
- Uber (2 Sept 2026) [primary announcement via Bloomberg]: micro-teams of one or two people cut ~50%; employees 7+ layers below the CEO reduced 20%; managers -20% — explicitly targeting the manager-of-tiny-team layer. — [Transport Topics/Bloomberg](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/uber-cut-3300-jobs-management); WSJ (3 Sept 2026, Chip Cutter): "managers with small fiefdoms have become an especially vulnerable target." — [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/corporate-america-is-axing-the-micro-team-boss-173d3e4e) (via [elinfonet excerpt](https://elinfonet.com/hr-headlines/corporate-america-is-axing-the-micro-team-boss-34417/))
- Cloudflare (May 2026) [primary statement via TechCrunch]: cuts fell on "measurers" — middle management, finance, legal, audit — not first-line builders. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/the-running-list-major-tech-layoffs-in-2026-where-employers-cited-ai/)
- Meta (May–Sept 2026) [secondary]: 2026 cuts "disproportionately affected managers"; the September re-creation of manager roles is in one engineering division (Applied AI), implying the removed coordination capacity was missed closest to the work. — [Fortune, 12 Sept 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/meta-year-of-efficiency-managers-ai-investment/)
- HBR Shin & Sucher (26 June 2026) [primary, qualitative; consulting firms]: the squeezed tier is the engagement/project-manager layer between partners and juniors — functionally first-line managers of AI-using ICs, but titled "middle managers." — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/06/ai-adoption-is-overloading-your-middle-managers)
- McKinsey State of AI 2026 [primary]: "47 percent of mid-level managers and individual contributors say they have experienced one of these negative effects, compared with 31 percent of executives and senior managers" — mid-level managers are grouped with ICs, not with senior managers. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai) (via search confirmation)
- Founder Reports (27 May 2026) [self-report]: managers 57% vs ICs 38% have fixed/redone over-AI-reliant colleague work; VPs/C-suite 63% — a seniority gradient in rework rather than a first-line spike. — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/ai-is-adding-to-your-managers-workload/)
- DDI (27 Jan 2026) [primary assessment data]: only 15% of emerging/frontline leaders strong at facilitating change (39% need significant development) vs 30% of mid-level leaders strong at leading change and 8% of executives. (2025 context: frontline managers 3x more likely than senior leaders to have AI concerns.) — [PR Newswire, Jan 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ddi-data-reveals-only-8-of-executives-are-strong-at-leading-through-change-302669190.html); [DDI blog](https://www.ddi.com/blog/ai-and-leadership)
- Gallup SOGW 2026 [primary]: "leaders" (middle-to-upper) report higher daily stress (46%), anger (33%), sadness (34%) and loneliness (31%) than ICs; the report does not isolate first-line supervisors. — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/349484/state-of-the-global-workplace.aspx)
- (2024 context via HBR Feb 2026) Gartner April 2024: HR leaders satisfied with effectiveness of mid-level managers 35%, frontline managers 27%. — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/02/stop-promoting-the-wrong-people-into-manager-roles)
- Korn Ferry Workforce 2026 [primary]: 79% of CEOs vs 51% of ICs report improved efficiencies from AI — a top-vs-bottom perception gap that middle layers must reconcile. — [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/about-us/press/korn-ferry-workforce-2026-report-unlocking-growth-requires-rethinking-how-work-gets-done)

### Inferences
- Job-security risk in 2026 concentrates on the "pure coordinator" middle layer (managers of managers, small-team bosses, "measurers"); workload and burnout risk concentrates on first-line/player-coach managers who both produce and review, and who inherit teams enlarged by delayering (Gallup's growth in 25+ teams).
- Because 2026 delayering converts many managers to ICs rather than exiting them (Google 2025, Uber 2026), the first-line tier is partly being refilled by demoted middle managers, which may raise the IC-load share and lower engagement further per Gallup's 40% threshold finding.
- The HBR consulting case shows the "middle manager" label in 2026 journalism often denotes the first accountable reviewer of IC output, so tier labels across sources are not directly comparable.

### Gaps
- No 2026 quantitative source reports separate headcount, span, engagement or burnout series for first-line vs middle managers; Gallup, Korn Ferry, McKinsey and Microsoft each use a single "manager" or blended "leader" category.
- No 2026 evidence on whether demoted middle managers who became ICs returned to management elsewhere, except Meta's one-division reversal.
- No source quantifies escalations or dashboards reaching managers at each tier.
