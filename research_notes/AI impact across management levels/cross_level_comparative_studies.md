## Key Question 1: Which 2026 studies report AI adoption or usage frequency broken down by seniority, and what are the numbers?

### Takeaway
Every 2026 study that splits adoption by level finds a monotonic gradient: senior leaders use AI most, managers next, ICs least, and the gap has widened since 2023. Gallup's Q2 2026 U.S. data puts frequent use at 51% (leaders) / 36% (managers) / 26% (ICs); Section's June 2026 data shows C-suite respondents are more than twice as likely as ICs to have access to agent-capable tools, and the gap in access, training and strategy (e.g., 81% vs 28% with a formal AI strategy) is far larger than the gap in raw usage.

### Cited Findings
- Gallup (Jul 27, 2026; updated Sep 30, 2026; U.S. Gallup Panel): share using AI "frequently" (a few times a week or more) in Q2 2026 — leaders 51% (from 17% in 2023), managers 36% (from 15%), individual contributors 26% (from 9%). "Extremely positive" perceived impact of AI: leaders 21% vs ICs 13% (no manager figure given). Sample sizes not stated on page. — [Gallup](https://gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx)
- Gallup / Andy Kemp (Apr 12, 2026; fieldwork Feb 4–19, 2026; n=23,717 employed U.S. adults, probability-based Gallup Panel, MOE ±0.9 pts): among employees whose organizations make AI tools available, frequent use is 67% for leaders, 52% for managers, 50% for project managers, 46% for ICs. Employees who strongly agree their manager actively supports AI use are 9.3x as likely to strongly agree AI has transformed how work gets done. — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/704252/workplace-separates-adopters-holdouts.aspx)
- Gallup (Jul 2026 page): 36% of employees in AI-integrating organizations strongly agree their manager actively supports their team's AI use; 78% of those who strongly agree use AI frequently vs 44% of those who do not. — [Gallup](https://gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx)
- 2025 context (reported in early-2026 secondary coverage of Gallup Q4 2025 data): use at least a few times a year was 69% leaders / 55% managers / 40% ICs; frequent use 44% / 30% / 23%; ~7 in 10 AI-using leaders say AI made them more efficient vs just over half of ICs. — [Wiss summary of Gallup](https://wiss.com/ai-adoption-at-work-in-2026-new-gallup-data/); [Allwork.Space, Jan 2026](https://allwork.space/2026/01/half-of-workers-never-use-ai-according-to-new-gallup-report/)
- Section "AI Proficiency Report 2026" (June 2026; n=5,026 U.S. knowledge workers): 67% use AI at least weekly (up from 55% in Oct 2025) but only 5.5% reach "Proficient"/"Expert"; 16% use an agentic tool; C-suite respondents are "more than twice as likely as ICs" to have access to agent-capable tools; employees whose managers expect AI use score 1.5x higher on proficiency and are 2.7x more likely to be excited; only 7% of managers tie AI use to performance reviews. — [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/ai/ai-proficiency-report-2026)
- Section Jan 2026 edition (n≈5,000 white-collar workers, US/UK/Canada, firms with 1,000+ employees; fieldwork reported as Sep–Nov 2025): C-suite vs ICs — formal AI strategy 81% vs 28%; tool access 80% vs 32%; company training 81% vs 27%; reimbursed for AI tools 63% vs 7%; "widespread adoption and sharing" 71% vs 8%; 94% of executives trust AI output and only 2% avoid it for work; manager encouragement for AI experimentation fell 11% since May 2025. (Figures via secondary write-up; exec sub-sample size not given.) — [Implicator.ai, Jan 22/Apr 13, 2026](https://www.implicator.ai/the-ai-productivity-mirage-executives-see-eight-hours-saved-workers-see-almost-nothing.md); methodology per [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/most-employees-using-ai-saving-less-than-half-workday-per-week)
- BCG AI at Work 2026 (Jun 3, 2026; ~12,000 frontline employees, managers and leaders, 14 markets): 74% of frontline employees use AI daily or several times a week, up 23 points from 2025; 84% have heard of AI agents, 30% say their organization has integrated agents into workflows (13% in 2025). Regular-use rates for managers/leaders are not broken out in the text (slides likely contain them). — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-at-work-why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools)
- Microsoft WTI 2026 (May 5, 2026; n=20,000 AI-using knowledge workers, 10 markets, Edelman DxI, Feb 18–Apr 7/20, 2026): the "Frontier Professional" top segment (16% of AI users, n=3,233) is 44% business decision makers and 56% employees; Frontier Professionals report managers who openly use AI at 85% vs 64% for others. Copilot telemetry: active agents grew 15x YoY (18x in large enterprises), Mar 2025–Mar 2026. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)
- Axios (Apr 13, 2026) coverage of a Gallup Workforce Panel analysis by Christos Makridis: frequent use "follows the org chart" — senior leaders in the low 40s, supervisors/project managers close behind, ICs in the low 20s. Page returned 403 on fetch; figures from search snippet only, unverified. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/13/ai-workplace-use-leaders-poll)
- Indeed/YouGov UK (date not confirmed as 2026): 57% of senior managers actively use AI at work vs 45% of employees. Unverified. — via [ADP/search summary](https://au.adp.com/about-adp/press-centre/half-of-global-workforce-using-ai-weekly-yet-frequent-users-question-their-productivity.aspx)
- ADP Research "People at Work 2026" (May 14, 2026; n>39,000 workers, 36 markets; fieldwork Jul 21–Aug 4, 2025): half of workers use AI multiple times a week, 20% nearly daily; daily users are 4x more likely than non-users to feel less productive; 11% of daily users report negative stress vs 23% of non-users. The press release does not break out managers vs ICs despite sampling both. — [ADP Research](https://au.adp.com/about-adp/press-centre/half-of-global-workforce-using-ai-weekly-yet-frequent-users-question-their-productivity.aspx)
- PwC Global Workforce Hopes and Fears 2026 (Sep 29, 2026; n=49,364 workers, 48 countries, May–Jun 2026): 64% used AI at work in the past year (+10 pts); daily GenAI use 14%→22%; 14% are AI "frontrunners"; 56% are "engine room" workers with less AI experience. Segmentation is by AI-skill tier, not management level. — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/companies-risk-losing-ai-savvy-employees.html)
- 2025 context (no 2026 Slack Workforce Index found): Slack Workforce Index (fieldwork Apr 9–May 1, 2025; n=5,156 desk workers, 6 countries) — daily AI users were 43% of executives, 35% of senior managers, 23% of middle managers. — [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/daily-ai-workforce-use-growth/)
- 2025 context: McKinsey "Superagency in the Workplace" (Jan 2025; 3,613 employees + 238 C-suite): C-suite estimated 4% of staff use AI for ≥30% of daily work; employees self-reported 13%. McKinsey's 2026 "State of AI" is an organization-level survey and no 2026 McKinsey employee-vs-leader usage comparison was found. — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-mckinsey-surveyed-c-suite-executives-and-found-they-believed-only-4-of-their-staff-used-ai-for-at-least-30-of-daily-work-the-real-figure-from-the-employees-themselves-was-13-meaning-ai-ha/)

### Inferences
- The adoption gradient is consistent across independent U.S. (Gallup), global (BCG, Section) and 2025 (Slack) datasets, so it is unlikely to be a sampling artifact; Gallup's own explanation is that AI tools fit leaders' desk-based, communication-heavy tasks better.
- Gallup's two 2026 numbers differ (51/36/26 vs 67/52/50/46) because the April figure is conditioned on the organization making AI tools available; the conditional gap (21 pts leader–IC) is smaller than the unconditional gap (25 pts), implying part of the IC shortfall is access, not preference.
- Section's access/strategy gaps (e.g., 81% vs 28% formal strategy) dwarf the usage gaps, suggesting much of the level difference is organizational provisioning rather than individual willingness.

### Gaps
- No 2026 Slack Workforce Index, Asana Work Innovation Lab, Grammarly/Harris Poll or EY Work Reimagined edition with level breakdowns was found; all surfaced editions were 2024–2025.
- BCG, ADP, PwC and Atlassian sampled multiple levels but their public text does not report adoption by manager vs IC; the figures likely sit in slide decks/full PDFs not accessed.
- Pew's 2026 surveys are not workplace-by-level; Pew's latest worker survey (Sept 2025 fieldwork) segments by education/age only.

## Key Question 2: Which 2026 studies report time saved, hours worked, or workload change by level? Do senior people report larger gains than junior people?

### Takeaway
Yes, decisively in self-report data: senior people report much larger time savings and efficiency gains than ICs (Section: >40% of C-level save 8+ hrs/week vs two-thirds of non-managers saving <2 hrs or nothing; Korn Ferry: 79% of CEOs vs 51% of ICs report efficiency gains). The one dataset that found roughly flat gains across ICs and managers (Workday) also found ~40% of gross savings lost to rework, and the only observational study (HBR/Berkeley) found AI increased workload via task expansion rather than reducing it.

### Cited Findings
- Section Jan 2026 edition via WSJ/Computerworld (Jan 23, 2026): over 40% of C-level executives say AI saves them more than eight hours per week; about two-thirds of non-management employees say it saves them less than two hours or no time; many non-managers describe AI as "stressful and difficult to use correctly" because output must be checked, corrected or redone; no separate manager figures. — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4121595/managers-and-employees-disagree-on-how-much-ai-improves-work-efficiency.html)
- Same Section study via Sherwood (Jan 22, 2026): 19% of executives say AI saved them more than 12 hours per week; 40% of nonmanagement workers say it saved no time at all. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/survey-ceos-and-workers-have-wildly-different-thoughts-on-ai-productivity/)
- Section (all respondents): 24% save zero time, 21% save under 2 hours (45% combined); 68% of AI-using employees in UK/North America save 4 hours or less per week. — [Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/the-ai-productivity-mirage-executives-see-eight-hours-saved-workers-see-almost-nothing.md); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/most-employees-using-ai-saving-less-than-half-workday-per-week)
- Korn Ferry Workforce 2026 (Sep 8, 2026; >16,000 professionals, 11 markets, IC to CEO): 51% of ICs report improved efficiencies from AI vs 79% of CEOs (28-pt gap); 62% of employees report significantly heavier workloads over the past two years; 44% stretched beyond capabilities; 61% perform responsibilities of more than one role; 52% of "AI-weary" workers say AI has increased their workloads; employee motivation fell from 71% (2024) to 61% (2026). — [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/about-us/press/korn-ferry-workforce-2026-report-unlocking-growth-requires-rethinking-how-work-gets-done)
- BCG AI at Work 2026 (Jun 3, 2026): among regular frontline AI users, 42% save at least eight hours (a full workday) per week — higher in marketing (60%), IT (53%), HR (50%); 66% of employees receive limited or no guidance on what to do with saved time; 60% say the bar for "good enough" work is higher; 41% say AI increased time spent on decisions; 48% of leaders report increased mental strain from AI vs 41% of all respondents; regular users reporting higher job satisfaction are concentrated among leaders. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-at-work-why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools)
- BCG (secondary coverage of same report): leaders reported the highest gains in enjoyment at work (77%) and the highest increase in cognitive load (48%); 65% of managers and leaders believe AI agents will take over at least half their jobs within three years (61% all respondents). — [Management Today](https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/six-ten-workers-think-ai-agents-half-job-within-three-years/indepth/article/1960280); [Consultancy.com.au](https://www.consultancy.com.au/news/12367/bcg-ai-is-reshaping-jobs-faster-than-companies-are-reshaping-work)
- Workday "Beyond Productivity" (Jan 14, 2026; 1,600 leaders + 1,600 employees, NA/EMEA/APAC, Nov 2025): 85% of employees report saving 1–7 hours/week; nearly 40% of AI time savings are lost to rework; only 14% consistently get clear positive net outcomes. Per No Jitter's coverage, roughly half of ICs reported gross savings of 1–3 hrs/week and 29% reported 4–7 hrs, and "the pattern was broadly consistent across individual contributors and managers"; employees spend up to two hours/week reviewing or correcting AI output. — [Workday newsroom](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-01-14-New-Workday-Research-Companies-Are-Leaving-AI-Gains-on-the-Table); [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/workday-report-finds-that-ai-helps-workers-reclaim-time)
- Workday UAE edition (2026): employees save 3.6 hrs/week with AI but spend 3.4 hrs clarifying, correcting or rewriting AI output; UK edition (May 14, 2026): one in four UK workers spend 7+ hours/week copying information between apps and feeding context to AI tools. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-employees-save-36-hours-a-week-using-ai-but-spend-34-hours-fixing-its-work); [Workday UK](https://en-gb.newsroom.workday.com/2026-05-14-New-Workday-Research-UK-Employees-Spend-Nearly-a-Full-Work-Day-Each-Week-Managing-Disconnected-AI-Tools?asPDF=1)
- HBR, Ranganathan & Ye (Feb 9, 2026; ~200 employees at one U.S. tech company, Apr–Dec 2025; observational/ethnographic, in-progress research): AI did not reduce work; it intensified it via (1) task expansion — designers and product managers performed engineering tasks they previously avoided, which added work for engineers who spent more time coaching colleagues and reviewing AI-assisted work; (2) blurred boundaries — AI use during lunches, meetings, breaks, spilling into evenings; (3) constant multitasking. Authors warn of "workload creep," cognitive fatigue and burnout. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/new-study-finds-ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it/); [Simon Willison, Feb 9, 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/9/ai-intensifies-work/); [ITPro](https://itpro.com/business/business-strategy/ai-isnt-making-work-easier-its-intensifying-it-researchers-say-teams-are-now-facing-unsustainable-workloads-cognitive-strain-and-higher-levels-of-burnout)
- Microsoft WTI 2026 (May 5, 2026): 66% of AI users say AI lets them spend more time on high-value work; 58% produce work they couldn't have a year ago (80% among Frontier Professionals); 65% fear falling behind if they don't adapt quickly; 45% say it feels safer to focus on current goals than redesign work with AI. No hours-saved or workload figures by level. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)
- WalkMe (Apr 9, 2026): 81% of executives say AI has significantly improved their productivity; employees lose 7.9 hours/week to digital frustrations; technology friction costs 51 working days per employee per year, up 42% from 36 days in 2025; 54% of workers bypassed AI tools and did tasks manually at least once in past 30 days; 33% of workers have not used AI at all. — [WalkMe](https://www.walkme.com/news-releases/enterprises-lose-51-workdays-per-employee-to-technology-friction-annually-despite-record-ai-investment-walkme-global-study-of-3750-finds)
- Gallup (Jul 2026): 65% of employees in AI-implementing organizations say AI has had a positive effect on productivity/efficiency (May 2026), not split by level; perceived productivity rises with breadth of use (45% at 1–2 purposes → 90% at 7+). — [Gallup](https://gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx)
- 2025 context: EY Work Reimagined 2025 (Aug 2025; 15,000 employees, 1,500 employers): 64% of employees perceive an increase in workload due to pressure to perform; only 12% receive sufficient AI training. No 2026 edition found. — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/11/ey-survey-reveals-companies-are-missing-out-on-up-to-40-percent-of-ai-productivity-gains-due-to-gaps-in-talent-strategy)
- 2024 context (often cited in 2026 coverage): Upwork Research Institute (Apr–May 2024; 2,500 workers incl. 1,250 managers): 96% of managers expected AI to boost productivity while 77% of AI-using employees said it added to their workload. Upwork's 2026 Future Workforce Index (Jul 14, 2026; 2,400 U.S. knowledge workers) has no level breakdown. — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/business/outdated-work-models-limit-ais-productivity-potential-upwork-study-42200); [Upwork 2026](https://www.upwork.com/press/releases/upworks-future-workforce-index-2026-how-ai-is-redefining-the-value-of-work-as-skilled-freelancing-accelerates)

### Inferences
- Senior respondents consistently report larger gains, but all level-split time-saved figures are self-reported; the only observational 2026 study (HBR) suggests saved time is reinvested as more work, and Workday's rework finding implies net savings for ICs may be near zero.
- Where time-saved is conditioned on being a regular user (BCG's 42% save 8+ hrs), frontline numbers look much better than Section's unconditional non-manager numbers — selection into regular use explains part of the level gap.
- Korn Ferry's pairing of "42% of organizations cut management roles" with "55% of remaining managers exhausted" and "39% of workers feel directionless" is the clearest 2026 evidence that workload effects on middle managers are driven by delayering as much as by AI tools themselves.

### Gaps
- No 2026 telemetry-based (non-survey) measurement of hours worked by level was found; Microsoft's 2026 WTI did not publish meeting/message/after-hours telemetry (see Q4).
- Section's manager tier is not reported separately in press coverage; the primary Jan 2026 report was not accessed.
- Workday's IC-vs-manager comparability claim rests on a single trade-press summary (No Jitter).

## Key Question 3: Which 2026 studies measure the "AI perception gap" / leader–employee disconnect, and how large is it?

### Takeaway
At least eight 2026 surveys quantify a leader–employee disconnect, and the gaps are large: 88% of executives vs 21% of employees say tools are adequate (WalkMe); 82% of C-suite vs 30% of ICs are excited about AI (Section, June 2026); 81% of leaders report productivity gains while 85% of employees report daily stress (BambooHR); 79% of CEOs vs 51% of ICs report efficiency gains (Korn Ferry). Executive self-perception also exceeds measured outcomes (Atlanta/Richmond Fed CFO data; NBER executive survey), so the gap is partly executive over-estimation, not only employee under-reporting.

### Cited Findings
- WalkMe State of Digital Adoption 2026 (Apr 9, 2026; 1,700 executives + 2,050 employees, 14 countries, 1,000+ employee enterprises): believe employees have adequate tools — 88% executives vs 21% employees; trust AI for complex business-critical decisions — 61% vs 9%; 81% of executives say AI significantly improved their productivity; 78% of executives want to discipline shadow-AI use while ≥45% of employees used unsanctioned AI in the past 30 days; 34% of employees don't know which AI tools are approved. (Vendor survey.) — [WalkMe](https://www.walkme.com/news-releases/enterprises-lose-51-workdays-per-employee-to-technology-friction-annually-despite-record-ai-investment-walkme-global-study-of-3750-finds)
- Section June 2026 (n=5,026 U.S.): 82% of C-suite describe themselves as excited about AI vs 30% of ICs. — [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/ai/ai-proficiency-report-2026)
- Section Jan 2026 via WSJ: more than 70% of executives "excited"; almost 70% of non-management workers "anxious or overwhelmed". — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/survey-ceos-and-workers-have-wildly-different-thoughts-on-ai-productivity/)

- The gap is two-sided: executives both use AI more (Q1) and over-attribute productivity relative to measured firm outcomes (Fed/NBER), while ICs report rework, stress and lack of guidance. - Vendor surveys (WalkMe, BambooHR, WRITER, Workday, Section) dominate the perception-gap literature; the non-vendor corroboration comes from Gallup, Korn Ferry, BCG, Microsoft (itself an AI vendor) and the Fed papers.
- Matched-item gaps (same question to both groups) are the most defensible: WalkMe 88/21 (tools), 61/9 (trust); Korn Ferry 79/51 (efficiency); Section 82/30 (excitement); Microsoft 81/67, 78/59, 21/10.

### Gaps
- Few studies ask leaders and employees the identical question about the employees' workload; most gaps are inferred from different items.
- No 2026 study measured the perception gap with three matched tiers (executive / middle manager / IC) on the same item except Gallup's frequency data and the Microsoft manager study (whose cross-level results are not published as a gap).

## Key Question 5: Which 2026 studies or labor-market datasets report job losses, hiring freezes or headcount changes attributable to AI by level?

### Takeaway
Administrative and postings data in 2026 agree that the burden is falling on the bottom of the hierarchy via reduced hiring, not layoffs: Stanford/ADP shows 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs 19% below trend (experienced workers show no gap); Chandar & Klein Teeselink find junior headcount −3% vs senior +6.7% at AI-adopting firms across 41 countries; Indeed shows senior postings +13.5% vs entry −6.3% and mid −6.7% since Jan 2025. Middle management is being cut (Korn Ferry: 42% of organizations cut management roles; Revelio: management postings −42% from 2022 peak), but no 2026 source attributes those cuts to AI with level-specific data, and the Challenger AI-layoff series (87,714 AI-attributed cuts YTD through May 2026) has no seniority breakdown. NY Fed and OECD researchers dispute AI causation for the entry-level slump.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (revised Aug 12, 2026; ADP payroll data through Jun 2026): (1) no evidence of widespread, economy-wide job displacement; (2) employment of workers aged 22–25 in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers (13% in the Aug 2025 original); experienced workers show no comparable gap; (3) divergence has widened steadily; (4) operates mainly through reduced hiring of young workers, not separations; (5) declines concentrate where AI substitutes for tasks; where AI complements, employment is flat or rising, especially for experienced workers; (6) adjustment via employment, not base pay. Robust to excluding tech firms, controlling for interest-rate exposure and remote work; weakens when controlling for education; some trends predate GenAI. Descriptive, not causal. — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/stanfords-brynjolfsson-study-entry-level-employment-in-ai-exposed-jobs-now-19)

## Key Question 6: Do any 2026 studies conclude AI's effects are roughly proportional across levels vs concentrated at particular levels? Strongest evidence on each side.

### Takeaway
The weight of 2026 evidence is that effects are concentrated, not proportional, but in opposite directions at the two ends: senior leaders capture the adoption, time-savings and enthusiasm gains, while entry-level workers absorb the labor-market cost (reduced hiring) and ICs absorb the stress/rework burden, with middle managers squeezed by delayering. The "proportional" case rests on NY Fed/OECD findings of no junior–senior divergence in postings, Workday's finding of similar gross hours saved across ICs and managers, and BCG's finding that cognitive load rises at every level (41% overall, 48% leaders).

### Cited Findings
Evidence for concentration (effects differ sharply by level):
- Gallup Q2 2026 frequent use 51% / 36% / 26% (leaders / managers / ICs), gap widened from 17/15/9 in 2023. — [Gallup](https://gallup.com/workplace/713063/ai-workplace-productivity.aspx)
- Section (Jan 2026): >40% of C-level save 8+ hrs/week; two-thirds of non-managers save <2 hrs or none; (Jun 2026): 82% C-suite vs 30% ICs excited. — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4121595/managers-and-employees-disagree-on-how-much-ai-improves-work-efficiency.html); [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/ai/ai-proficiency-report-2026)
- Korn Ferry 2026: 79% of CEOs vs 51% of ICs report efficiency gains; 42% of organizations cut management roles; 55% of remaining managers exhausted. — [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/about-us/press/korn-ferry-workforce-2026-report-unlocking-growth-requires-rethinking-how-work-gets-done)
- Stanford/ADP (Aug 2026): 19% employment shortfall for 22–25-year-olds in exposed occupations; "experienced workers show no comparable gap"; where AI complements, employment is flat or rising "especially for experienced workers." — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)
- Chandar & Klein Teeselink (Sep 2026): senior +6.7% vs junior −3% at adopters, 41 countries. — [The Star / Bloomberg](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/22/ai-adoption-is-driving-hiring-but-mostly-for-senior-roles)

- PwC 2026: benefits concentrating in a 14% "frontrunner" minority while the 56% "engine room" lacks training; press release headline "Companies risk losing their most AI-savvy employees while leaving majority behind." — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/companies-risk-losing-ai-savvy-employees.html)
- Microsoft WTI 2026: organizational factors (culture, manager support) explain 67% of AI-impact variance vs 32% individual; leader–employee gaps of 14–19 pts on psychological safety/experimentation. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)

Evidence for proportionality / no level divergence:
- NY Fed Audoly, Guerin & Topa (May 14, 2026): splitting high-exposure occupations into junior vs senior postings shows "no meaningful divergence"; AI not the main driver of the hiring slowdown. — [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/new-york-fed-data-says-ai-is-not-driving-the-hiring-slowdown/); [AI2Work](https://ai2.work/blog/new-york-fed-research-challenges-the-ai-blame-game-for-hiring-slowdown)
- NY Fed Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais (Jun 1, 2026): the youth unemployment gap persists when AI exposure is held constant; remote work explains ~64% of the rise. — [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/06/remote-work-leaves-younger-workers-sidelined/)
- OECD (Aug 2026): current evidence does not attribute the weaker youth labour market to AI. — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/blogs/2026/08/young-people-labour-markets-and-ai-what-oecd-evidence-shows.html)
- Indeed (Jul 2026) itself declines to attribute the seniority tilt to AI, noting remote work, rate hikes and post-pandemic correction. — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/)
- Workday (Jan 2026): gross hours-saved distribution "broadly consistent across individual contributors and managers." — [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/workday-report-finds-that-ai-helps-workers-reclaim-time)
- BCG 2026: cognitive load up at all levels (41% overall; leaders 48%); 61% of all respondents (65% of managers/leaders) think agents could do half their job within three years — the exposure fear is shared up the hierarchy. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/ai-at-work-why-strategy-matters-more-than-tools); [Management Today](https://www.managementtoday.co.uk/six-ten-workers-think-ai-agents-half-job-within-three-years/indepth/article/1960280)
- Stanford/ADP (Aug 2026) fact 1: "no evidence of widespread, economy-wide job displacement"; NBER exec survey (Mar 2026): >90% of firms report no measurable employment effect. — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/); [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/90percent-of-firms-see-no-measurable-ai-impact-on-productivity-or-jobs-study-51625)
- HBR Ranganathan & Ye (Feb 2026): intensification appeared across roles (designers, PMs, engineers) rather than being confined to one tier, though it redistributed review work toward engineers. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/tech/new-study-finds-ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it/)

### Inferences
- Self-report surveys uniformly show concentration of benefits at the top; administrative data shows concentration of labor-market costs at the bottom; the "proportional" evidence is mostly about causation (AI vs remote work/macro) rather than about the shape of outcomes by level, which almost all 2026 sources agree is tilted.
- The two positions are reconcilable: AI-adopting firms are hiring more overall (Revelio +27%, Chandar & Klein Teeselink) while shifting composition toward senior staff; postings-level studies that do not condition on firm adoption (NY Fed) may miss this within-firm compositional shift.
- Middle managers are the least-measured tier in 2026: usage sits between leaders and ICs (Gallup 36%), but their workload/burnout is driven by delayering (Korn Ferry) rather than by direct AI tool effects, and no study isolates AI's contribution.

### Gaps
- No 2026 study was found that explicitly frames and tests a "proportional vs concentrated" hypothesis across three or more levels on matched outcome measures; the conclusion above is synthesized from separate level-split findings.
- Observed (telemetry/administrative) data on productivity by level is absent; all productivity-by-level comparisons are self-reported.
